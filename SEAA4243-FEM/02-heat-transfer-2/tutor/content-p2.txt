# 2D Heat Transfer — Part 2
Galerkin and integration by parts turn the PDE into [k]{T̂}ᵀ = {q} + {b}; the element matrices in closed form, assembly by connectivity, the known temperatures imposed, and the text’s plate solved by hand and checked against a fine mesh.

[start-outcomes] After this part you should be able to
1. Apply the Galerkin weighted residual method and integration by parts to obtain [k]{T̂}ᵀ = {q} + {b} and explain what each term means.
2. Evaluate the conductance matrix [k] and the load vectors {q} and {b} of a 4-node rectangular element in closed form.
3. Assemble element matrices into the global system with a connectivity table and impose Dirichlet conditions by row–column elimination.
4. Solve a small problem by hand, post-process temperature and flux at a point, and judge the result against a refined mesh.

[notes-n1] Notes 1. Galerkin and integration by parts
Substitute the approximation T = {N}{T̂}ᵀ of Part 1 into the PDE and it does not balance: a bilinear field cannot satisfy a second-order equation at every point. The residual is what is left over. Four unknowns T̂_1…T̂_4, one equation, and it holds nowhere exactly.
EQUATION (2.34): [equation w_resid: source not available in the page]
Galerkin’s idea Ask instead that the residual be zero on average , weighted by each shape function in turn. Four weights, four equations. Galerkin chooses the weights equal to the shape functions, which makes [k] symmetric.

## Integration by parts: the 1D case you know
For the bar element earlier in the course, one derivative moved from T onto the weight and a boundary term appeared:
EQUATION : [equation w_ibp1d: source not available in the page]
- Purpose 1 · relax continuity. After the integration by parts only first derivatives of N and T appear, so a bilinear T is admissible. Its second derivatives would be zero or undefined at element edges.
- Purpose 2 · expose the natural condition. The boundary term contains k ∂T/∂n, the flux. Where the flux is specified it becomes a load; where it is zero it vanishes; where T is specified the term is removed with the equation.
## Two dimensions: Green’s theorem
The same move in two dimensions is Green’s theorem: the area integral of a derivative becomes a boundary integral minus an area integral with the derivative on the weight.

Term Name What it holds
[k] conductance matrix the material and the geometry of the element; symmetric; 4 × 4
{q} source vector the heat generated inside the element, shared among its nodes
{b} boundary vector the heat entering through edges with a known flux; zero on insulated edges; discarded with the equation on Dirichlet edges
Which edges contribute to {b}? For the text’s plate (top and left at 0 °C, right and bottom insulated): none. The insulated edges have q_b = 0, and on the two Dirichlet edges the unknown flux multiplies the N_i of nodes whose equations we discard when T is imposed. The flux there can be recovered afterwards, like a support reaction.
Key points:
- Galerkin: weighted residual with the shape functions as weights; integration by parts lowers the derivative order and exposes the natural condition.
- [k]{T̂}ᵀ = {q} + {b} = {r}: conductance matrix, source vector, boundary vector.
- One 4-node element gives 4 equations; the 2 × 2 mesh gives 9, and 4 remain after T is imposed on 5 nodes.
Quick check (not graded): In the Galerkin method the weighting functions are
Options: the shape functions themselves | the constants 1, x, x² | the nodal temperatures
Explanation shown after answering: Galerkin takes the weights equal to the trial functions, so the equations come out symmetric. Weights 1, x, x² give a Petrov–Galerkin (basic WRM) system that is not symmetric.

## The load vectors
A uniform source is shared equally among the four nodes, because every N_i has the same integral ab/4 over the element. A uniform inward flux on an edge is shared equally by the two nodes of that edge, because along the edge only their shape functions are non-zero and each integrates to half the length.

## Counting the equations
Nine nodes give nine equations. The top edge (nodes 7, 8, 9) and the left edge (nodes 1, 4, 7) are at 0 °C: five known temperatures, so four equations remain, for T_2, T_3, T_5, T_6.
Key points:
- Connectivity: global node numbers of each element in local anticlockwise order.
- K_{ii} = s_1 × (number of elements at node i); a shared side adds twice.
- Text plate: 9 equations, 5 known temperatures, 4 unknowns.

## Impose the known temperatures
A known T̄_k is not an unknown, so its equation is not needed and its column multiplies a known number. Move that column times T̄_k to the right-hand side, then delete the row and the column. Here all five known values are zero, so nothing moves to the right and the reduced system is simply rows and columns 2, 3, 5, 6.

[notes-n5] Notes 5. Post-processing and accuracy
The solution is a list of nodal values. Everything else — the temperature at a point, the flux, the hottest spot — is post-processing with the shape functions of Part 1, done one element at a time in that element’s local coordinates.
## Temperature at any point: three steps
- Find the element that contains the point.
- Convert to local coordinates: subtract the element’s origin (its local node 1).
- Evaluate N_1…N_4 there and multiply by the element’s nodal temperatures, in local order from the connectivity table.

[notes-n6] Notes 6. The script, the assignment and what comes next
The script is the pipeline in thirty lines. Read it against the seven steps and every block has a name.
% Local conductance matrix (text §2.3.1)
a = L/Nx; b = H/Ny;
s1 = b*kx/(3*a) + a*ky/(3*b);
s2 = -b*kx/(3*a) + a*ky/(6*b);
s3 = -b*kx/(6*a) - a*ky/(6*b);
s4 = b*kx/(6*a) - a*ky/(3*b);
k = [s1 s2 s3 s4; s2 s1 s4 s3; s3 s4 s1 s2; s4 s3 s2 s1];
fQ = a*b*Q/4*[1; 1; 1; 1]; % heat generation
% Assembly by connectivity (conn(e,:) = global nodes of element e)
for e = 1:Ne, c = conn(e,:); K(c,c) = K(c,c) + k; F(c) = F(c) + fQ; end
% Essential conditions: known nodes removed, their columns moved to the right
free = setdiff(1:n, fixed); T = zeros(n,1); T(fixed) = Tbar;
T(free) = K(free,free) \ (F(free) - K(free,fixed)*T(fixed));
% Post-processing: element, local coordinates, interpolation
e = find_element(x, y); xl = x - X(conn(e,1)); yl = y - Y(conn(e,1));
N = [(a-xl)*(b-yl), xl*(b-yl), xl*yl, yl*(a-xl)]/(a*b); Tp = N*T(conn(e,:));
- Lines 2–8: step 5, the element matrix and load from the closed-form s_1…s_4 of §2.
- Line 10: step 6, assembly, one scatter-add per element.
- Lines 12–13: step 6, the known temperatures moved to the right and struck; step 7, the backslash solve.
- Lines 15–16: §5, interpolation in local coordinates.
Try it Change the mesh to 4 × 4 and compare T(1, 0) with the table of §5. Then set Q = 0 and T = 5 on the left and 100 on the right edge: every mesh must return a straight line.

[glossary-residual] Glossary: residual
residual: What is left when the approximation is substituted into the PDE; zero only for the exact solution.

[glossary-weighted-residual] Glossary: weighted residual
weighted residual: The residual multiplied by a weight and integrated; set to zero to get one equation per weight.

[glossary-galerkin-method] Glossary: Galerkin method
Galerkin method: Weighted residual with the shape functions as weights; gives a symmetric [k].

[glossary-integration-by-parts-green-s-theorem] Glossary: integration by parts · Green’s theorem
integration by parts · Green’s theorem: Moves one derivative from T onto the weight and produces a boundary term; in 2D the area integral trades with a boundary integral.

[glossary-weak-form] Glossary: weak form
weak form: The integrated, lower-derivative statement that the finite element equations come from.

[glossary-conductance-matrix-k] Glossary: conductance matrix [k]
conductance matrix [k]: 4 × 4, symmetric, from ∫{N}ᵀ{∂}[E]{∂}ᵀ{N} dA; the heat analogue of stiffness. Units W K^{−1} per unit thickness.

[glossary-s-1-s-4] Glossary: s_1 …s_4
s_1 …s_4: The four distinct entries of [k] for a rectangle, (2.49); s_1 diagonal, s_2 horizontal side, s_4 vertical side, s_3 diagonal pairs.

[glossary-source-vector-q] Glossary: source vector {q}
source vector {q}: Nodal loads from the heat generated inside the element, q_h ab/4 each for a uniform source.

[glossary-boundary-vector-b] Glossary: boundary vector {b}
boundary vector {b}: Nodal loads from a known flux on an edge, q_b ℓ/2 at each of the two nodes of that edge.

[glossary-row-sum-check] Glossary: row-sum check
row-sum check: Every row of [k] adds to zero because a uniform temperature conducts no heat.

[glossary-connectivity-table] Glossary: connectivity table
connectivity table: For each element, its global node numbers in local anticlockwise order.

[glossary-assembly-scatter-add] Glossary: assembly · scatter-add
assembly · scatter-add: K_{IJ} += k_{ij} with I, J read from the connectivity table; the same for {R}.

[glossary-global-system-k-t-r] Glossary: global system [K]{T} = {R}
global system [K]{T} = {R}: All elements assembled; singular until at least one temperature is prescribed.

[glossary-row-column-elimination] Glossary: row–column elimination
row–column elimination: Imposing a known T̄: move its column × T̄ to the right-hand side, delete the row and the column.

[glossary-reduced-system] Glossary: reduced system
reduced system: The equations that remain for the unknown temperatures after the known ones are struck.

[glossary-struck-rows-energy-balance] Glossary: struck rows · energy balance
struck rows · energy balance: Rows of the known nodes, evaluated afterwards, give the heat flows there; their sum balances the heat put in.

[glossary-local-coordinates] Glossary: local coordinates
local coordinates: Position measured from an element’s local node 1; what N_i must be evaluated with.

[glossary-post-processing] Glossary: post-processing
post-processing: Everything computed from the nodal solution: T at a point, flux, hottest point, heat flows.

[glossary-mesh-refinement-convergence] Glossary: mesh refinement · convergence
mesh refinement · convergence: Repeating the solution on finer meshes until the answer stops changing; bilinear elements converge as h².

[glossary-relative-error] Glossary: relative error
relative error: (T_{mesh} − T_{ref} )/T_{ref} ; about +5% for the 2 × 2 mesh of the text’s plate.

[glossary-patch-test] Glossary: patch test
patch test: A field the element can represent exactly (here a linear T) must be reproduced exactly on any mesh.

[glossary-symmetry] Glossary: symmetry
symmetry: Equal geometry, material, loads and boundary conditions about a line give equal temperatures at mirror nodes; a line of symmetry carries no heat across it.
