Why plane stress?

Thin bodies loaded in their own plane: shear walls, deep beams, gusset plates, the web of a girder. Two unknowns at every point, u and v.

SEAA 4243 · Plane Stress — Part 1

5

Block 1 · the modelling assumption · text §3.1

Thin and loaded in-plane: plane stress. Long with a constant section: plane strain

Plane stress: the thickness t is small and nothing acts on the faces, so σzz = σxz = σyz = 0. The body may thin or thicken freely (εzz ≠ 0).

Plane strain: the body is long and every section is loaded the same way, so εzz = 0 and a stress σzz = ν(σxx + σyy) develops to prevent it.

Plane stress in practice

  • Shear walls, deep beams
  • Gusset plates, connection plates
  • Web of a plate girder, thin flanges
  • Thin slabs loaded in their plane

Plane strain in practice

  • Gravity dams, long embankments
  • Tunnel linings, pipes, culverts
  • Retaining walls (per metre run)
  • Strip footings

Block 1 · the unknowns

Displacements u(x, y), v(x, y); from them strains, then stresses

Heat transfer carried one unknown per node. Plane stress carries two: the nodal displacements ui and vi. A 4-node element has 8 degrees of freedom and an 8 × 8 stiffness matrix.

QuantityHeat transferPlane stress
primary fieldT (scalar)u, v (vector)
derived fieldflux qx, qystrains ε, stresses σ
material lawFourier: q = −k∇THooke: {σ} = [E]{ε}
dof per node12
4-node [k]4 × 48 × 8

Why

Displacement-based FEM: we solve for u and v, then differentiate to get strains and stresses — so stresses are one order less accurate than displacements.

Block 3 · the plane-strain matrix, for contrast

Same shape, different constants — and ε₍zz₎ = 0 instead of σ₍zz₎ = 0

from 3D Hooke’s law with ε_zz = 0

Why it matters

Choosing plane stress for a dam (or plane strain for a thin plate) changes the stiffness by a factor of order 1/(1 − ν²): several percent for steel, more for rubber-like materials. ANSYS asks you which one; the FEM machinery does not care.

In this course

We formulate and hand-calculate plane stress (text Chapter 3). Plane strain is the same [k] with this [E] substituted — one line in the MATLAB code.

Block 5 · eight degrees of freedom · text Fig. 3.2

Each node carries u (along x) and v (along y); the dof vector interleaves them

(3.24), (3.25)

Ordering convention

  • {û} = {u1 v1 u2 v2 u3 v3 u4 v4}: node by node, u before v.
  • Global: node n has dofs U2n−1 (x) and U2n (y).
  • The same Ni(x, y) as (2.21) interpolate both u and v.

Watch out

Keep this ordering everywhere: in [N], in [B], in [k] and in the connectivity. Mixing “all u then all v” with “node by node” is the classic assembly error.
