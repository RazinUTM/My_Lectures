# Plane Stress — Part 1
Two unknowns at every point: equilibrium, Hooke’s law and strain–displacement give [∂][E][∂]ᵀ{u}ᵀ = −{f}; then the boundary conditions and the element’s 2 × 8 shape-function matrix [N].

[start-outcomes] After this part you should be able to
1. Derive the plane-stress equilibrium equations from a differential element and write them as [∂]{σ}ᵀ = −{f}.
2. State the plane-stress constitutive law {σ}ᵀ = [E]{ε}ᵀ and the strain–displacement relation {ε}ᵀ = [∂]ᵀ{u}ᵀ, contrast plane stress with plane strain, and combine them into [∂][E][∂]ᵀ{u}ᵀ = −{f}.

[notes-n1] Notes 1. Why plane stress: two unknowns at every point
Heat transfer gave a scalar field: one temperature at every point and one unknown at every node. Plane stress gives a vector field: every point of a plate moves by a displacement with two components, u(x, y) along x and v(x, y) along y. From the displacements we get strains, and from the strains stresses. The finite element method solves for u and v at the nodes; strains and stresses come afterwards, by differentiation.
[Figure: Left: heat transfer, one number per node. Right: plane stress, two numbers per node, u and v.]
Plane stress and plane strain Plane stress: the body is thin (thickness t small) and loaded only in its own plane, so its faces are free: σ_{zz} = σ_{xz} = σ_{yz} = 0, while the plate may thin or thicken (ε_{zz} ≠ 0). Plane strain: the body is long in z and every section is loaded alike, so each slice is held by its neighbours: ε_{zz} = 0, and a stress σ_{zz} = ν(σ_{xx} + σ_{yy}) develops to keep it so.
[Figure: Plane stress (thin plate, free faces) and plane strain (long body, every slice alike).]
## Where civil engineers use each
Plane stress Plane strain
shear walls, deep beams gravity dams, long embankments
gusset and connection plates tunnel linings, pipes, culverts
webs of plate girders retaining walls, per metre run
slabs loaded in their own plane strip footings
A slab carrying a floor load is neither: the load acts normal to its plane, so it bends as a plate, a different problem. The test for plane stress is always the same two questions: is the body thin compared with its in-plane size, and do the loads act in its plane?

## What the finite element model solves for
Heat transfer Plane stress
primary field T (scalar) u, v (vector)
derived field flux q_x, q_y strains ε, stresses σ
material law Fourier: q = −k∇T Hooke: {σ} = [E]{ε}
dofs per node 1 2
4-node [k] 4 × 4 8 × 8
Displacement-based FEM We solve for u and v and differentiate them to get strains and stresses. Each differentiation costs accuracy, so stresses are always less accurate than displacements, and they jump between elements. Part 3 is about exactly that.
Key points:
- Plane stress: thin, loaded in plane, σ_{zz} = 0. Plane strain: long, uniform section, ε_{zz} = 0.
- Two unknowns per node, u and v; a 4-node element has 8 dofs and an 8 × 8 [k].
- Strains and stresses come from derivatives of the displacements: less accurate.

[notes-n2] Notes 2. Equilibrium of a differential element
Take a small rectangle dx by dy of unit thickness inside the plate. On its faces act the normal stresses σ_{xx} and σ_{yy} and the shear stresses σ_{xy} and σ_{yx} (N m^{−2}); inside acts a body force f_x, f_y per unit volume (N m^{−3}), for example self-weight f_y = −ρg. The method is the one used for the heat flux in Heat Transfer Part 1: the stress on a far face is the near-face stress plus its first-order change.
[Figure: The differential element: normal and shear stresses on the faces, body force inside (text Fig. 3.1).]
Sign convention A stress component is positive when it acts in the positive coordinate direction on a face whose outward normal points in a positive direction, and in the negative direction on a face whose outward normal is negative.

Why σ_{yx} = σ_{xy} Take moments about the centre of the element: the pair σ_{xy} dy on the x-faces and the pair σ_{yx} dx on the y-faces each make a couple with arm dx or dy. Equilibrium of moments makes the two couples equal, σ_{xy} dy dx = σ_{yx} dx dy, so the shear stresses are equal. Only three stress components remain.

## Worked step · Activity 2 in words
Forces in y: σ_{yy} on the bottom face (area dx) acts downward, σ_{yy} + ∂σ_{yy}/∂y dy on the top face acts upward; σ_{xy} on the left face (area dy) acts downward, σ_{xy} + ∂σ_{xy}/∂x dx on the right face acts upward; f_y dx dy acts on the volume. Sum, cancel, divide by dx dy: ∂σ_{xy}/∂x + ∂σ_{yy}/∂y + f_y = 0. Row 2 of [∂] is therefore [0 ∂/∂y ∂/∂x].
Key points:
- Force balance in x and y on dx × dy gives (3.4) and (3.6); moment balance gives σ_{yx} = σ_{xy}.
- Matrix form [∂]{σ}ᵀ = −{f} with the 2 × 3 operator [∂].
- Every term is a force per unit volume, N m^{−3}.

Engineering shear strain The text’s ε_{xy} is the engineering shear strain, ε_{xy} = ∂u/∂y + ∂v/∂x (written γ_{xy} in most solid-mechanics books), not the tensor strain, which is half of it. The (3,3) entry of [E] is built for it: (E/(1 − ν²))·(1 − ν)/2 = E/(2(1 + ν)) = G, the shear modulus.

## Plane strain changes only [E]
Setting ε_{zz} = 0 instead of σ_{zz} = 0 in three-dimensional Hooke’s law gives a matrix with the same shape and different constants:

The shear entry is G in both. The normal entries are larger in plane strain, because a slice that cannot thin resists in-plane stretching more. For ν = 0.33 the bending stiffness of the cantilever rises by the factor 1/(1 − ν²) = 1.12, so its tip deflection falls to about 0.89 of the plane-stress value; the Explorer shows 0.90 on a fine mesh. [notes-n4] Notes 4. Strain–displacement, and the PDE in u and v
Heat transfer had no step like this one, because T was itself the unknown. Here the unknowns are displacements, and the strains are their derivatives: ε_{xx} measures stretching along x, ε_{yy} along y, and ε_{xy} the change of a right angle.

Condition Known In the FEM Heat analogue
displacement · essential ū, v̄ imposed on the assembled system (row–column elimination) T̄

Count per direction, not per edge A roller edge is essential in one direction and natural in the other. A free edge is not a support and not a load: it is the do-nothing condition, exactly like an insulated edge. Adding a support where none exists is the commonest wrong model in ANSYS.

## Why the supports must stop every rigid motion
A plate can translate in x, translate in y and rotate in its plane without straining at all: for a small rotation θ, u = −θy and v = θx give ε_{xx} = ε_{yy} = 0 and ε_{xy} = −θ + θ = 0. Such a motion needs no force, so [K] is singular until the supports remove all three. Holding v alone along the left edge stops the y-translation but not the x-translation, nor a rotation about a point of that edge (its points move in x, not in y); the Explorer’s “vertical only” support shows the solver refusing. Holding u alone along the edge stops x-translation and rotation but leaves the plate free to slide in y.
Key points:
- Traction known → natural → load (or nothing on a free edge). Displacement known → essential → imposed.
- One condition per direction at every boundary point.
- Supports must remove two translations and one rotation, or [K] is singular.

[glossary-plane-stress] Glossary: plane stress
plane stress: A thin body loaded in its own plane: σ_{zz} = σ_{xz} = σ_{yz} = 0, while ε_{zz} may be non-zero.

[glossary-plane-strain] Glossary: plane strain
plane strain: A long body with every section loaded alike: ε_{zz} = 0, and σ_{zz} = ν(σ_{xx} + σ_{yy} ) develops.

[glossary-displacement-u-v] Glossary: displacement u, v
displacement u, v: The movement of a point along x and along y; the primary unknowns of plane stress.

[glossary-normal-stress-shear-stress] Glossary: normal stress · shear stress
normal stress · shear stress: σ_{xx} , σ_{yy} act normal to a face; σ_{xy} = σ_{yx} act along it (N m^{−2} ).

[glossary-body-force-f] Glossary: body force f
body force f: Force per unit volume acting inside the body, N m^{−3} ; self-weight f_y = −ρg.

[glossary-differential-element] Glossary: differential element
differential element: A small rectangle dx × dy on which equilibrium is written before the size goes to zero.

[glossary-equilibrium-equations] Glossary: equilibrium equations
equilibrium equations: (3.4) and (3.6): the stress gradients balance the body force in x and in y.

[glossary-operator-matrix] Glossary: operator matrix [∂]
operator matrix [∂]: The 2 × 3 matrix of derivatives in [∂]{σ}ᵀ = −{f}; its transpose gives the strains.

[glossary-constitutive-matrix-e] Glossary: constitutive matrix [E]
constitutive matrix [E]: The 3 × 3 matrix of Hooke’s law, {σ}ᵀ = [E]{ε}ᵀ; different for plane stress and plane strain.

[glossary-young-s-modulus-e-poisson-s-ratio] Glossary: Young’s modulus E · Poisson’s ratio ν
Young’s modulus E · Poisson’s ratio ν: Stiffness in uniaxial tension; lateral contraction per unit axial strain.

[glossary-shear-modulus-g] Glossary: shear modulus G
shear modulus G: G = E/(2(1 + ν)); the (3,3) entry of [E] in both idealisations.

[glossary-engineering-shear-strain-xy] Glossary: engineering shear strain ε_{xy}
engineering shear strain ε_{xy}: ∂u/∂y + ∂v/∂x, the change of a right angle (γ_{xy} in many books); twice the tensor shear strain.

[glossary-strain-displacement-relation] Glossary: strain–displacement relation
strain–displacement relation: {ε}ᵀ = [∂]ᵀ{u}ᵀ: three strains from two displacements.

[glossary-traction-b] Glossary: traction b
traction b: Force per unit area on the boundary, b_x = σ_{xx} n_x + σ_{xy} n_y , b_y = σ_{xy} n_x + σ_{yy} n_y .

[glossary-natural-essential] Glossary: natural · essential
natural · essential: Natural: a known traction, entered as a load. Essential: a known displacement, imposed on the assembled system.

[glossary-free-edge] Glossary: free edge
free edge: A natural condition with zero traction; the finite element model does nothing there.

[glossary-rigid-body-motion] Glossary: rigid-body motion
rigid-body motion: Translation or rotation without strain; supports must remove all three in 2D or [K] is singular.

[glossary-reaction] Glossary: reaction
reaction: Force at a support, recovered after solving from the struck rows of the system.

[glossary-degree-of-freedom-dof] Glossary: degree of freedom (dof)
degree of freedom (dof): One nodal unknown; node n owns U_{2n−1} (x) and U_{2n} (y).

[glossary-shape-function-matrix-n] Glossary: shape-function matrix [N]
shape-function matrix [N]: The 2 × 8 matrix with {u v}ᵀ = [N]{û}ᵀ; the same N_i for u and for v.

[glossary-strain-displacement-matrix-b] Glossary: strain-displacement matrix [B]
strain-displacement matrix [B]: [B] = [∂]ᵀ[N], 3 × 8, so that {ε}ᵀ = [B]{û}ᵀ; built in Part 2.

[glossary-stiffness-matrix-k] Glossary: stiffness matrix [k]
stiffness matrix [k]: [k] = t∫[B]ᵀ[E][B] dA, 8 × 8 for the four-node element; the analogue of the conductance matrix.
