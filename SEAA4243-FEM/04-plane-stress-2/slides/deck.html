Block 1 · the strain–displacement matrix [B] · text §3.3

[B] = [∂]ᵀ[N]: strains from the nodal displacements

Why

Row 1: ∂Ni/∂x under each ui. Row 2: ∂Ni/∂y under each vi. Row 3: ∂Ni/∂y under ui, ∂Ni/∂x under vi.

Check

Each entry is a Heat Transfer Part 1 derivative: ∂N1/∂x = −(b − y)/ab = (y − b)/ab, and so on. Nothing new is computed.

SEAA 4243 · Plane Stress — Part 2

Block 1 · the stiffness matrix in [B] form

The same [B] twice, with [E] between them, integrated over the element and multiplied by the thickness

Why the thickness appears

The integrals of (3.35) are per unit thickness; the physical element has thickness t, so every entry of [k] and every load is multiplied by t. The text keeps t inside s1…s12.

Why it is symmetric

[B]ᵀ[E][B] is symmetric whenever [E] is. Integration preserves symmetry. The 8 × 8 matrix therefore has at most 36 distinct entries — and by the rectangle’s symmetry only 12.

What is being computed

kij = force at dof i per unit displacement of dof j, all others held. It is the virtual-work pairing of the strain patterns of dofs i and j through Hooke’s law.

Block 2 · how software integrates [k]

Software adds [B]ᵀ[E][B] at four Gauss points

Image: PyMAPDL documentation, “Build a finite element model” (how a finite element works), © ANSYS, Inc., MIT licence.

What the program does

  • Evaluate [B] at ξ, η = ±1/√3 (the orange points).
  • Form t [B]ᵀ[E][B] × det J at each point; weights are 1.
  • Add the four: that is [k], 8 × 8.

Check

For our rectangle the four-point sum equals the closed form (3.42) to machine precision: we checked it for the cantilever element.

Why

For a distorted element like this one there is no closed form, so numerical integration in ξ, η is the only route. That is why software works on the square.

Block 4 · the assembled [K], 18 × 18 · text (3.46)

Each entry is filled by the elements that share both its dofs

Read the map

  • Diagonal blocks of node 5 (U9, U10) are shared by all four elements: the number 4.
  • Edge nodes are shared by two elements; corner nodes by one.
  • Zero where the two dofs belong to nodes that no element has in common: node 1 and node 9 never meet.

Why

Bandwidth: the non-zeros stay close to the diagonal because neighbouring nodes have nearby numbers. Numbering across the short side first keeps the band narrow — software renumbers for you.

Antisymmetric bending, a neutral axis, and a mesh that is too stiff

Timoshenko beam: bending + shear

Watch out

Block 5 · deflected shapes

The hand mesh deflects two thirds as much as the converged mesh

What the picture shows

  • Both meshes bend; the coarse one bends less.
  • The bilinear element cannot curve: each element’s edges stay straight, so bending is represented by a kink pattern.
  • Straight edges force spurious shear strain under bending — the element resists with shear stiffness it should not have: shear locking.

Why

More elements → smaller kinks → less spurious shear. The 8-node element (Plane Stress Part 3) curves instead.

Block 6 · the script, as a diff against Heat Transfer Part 2

Four changes turn the heat-transfer script into the plane-stress script

% ---- changes from the heat script ----

E = 200e6; nu = 0.33; t = 0.01; qy = 5000;

Nx = 2; Ny = 2; ndof = 18; % 2 dofs per node

a = Lx/Nx; b = Ly/Ny;

 

% 12 closed-form values, text (3.42)

s1 = (E*t*((1-nu)*a^2 + 2*b^2))/(6*(1-nu^2)*a*b);

s2 = (E*t*((1-nu) + 2*nu))/(8*(1-nu^2));

s3 = (E*t*((1-nu)*a^2 - 4*b^2))/(12*(1-nu^2)*a*b);

% ... s4 to s12 likewise ...

k = [s1 s2 s3 s4 s5 s6 s7 s8; ...]; % 8 x 8, (3.42)

% assembly with the dof map (node n -> 2n-1, 2n)

K = zeros(ndof); F = zeros(ndof,1);

d1 = [1 2 3 4 9 10 7 8]; K(d1,d1) = K(d1,d1) + k;

d2 = [3 4 5 6 11 12 9 10]; K(d2,d2) = K(d2,d2) + k;

d3 = [7 8 9 10 15 16 13 14]; K(d3,d3) = K(d3,d3) + k;

d4 = [9 10 11 12 17 18 15 16]; K(d4,d4) = K(d4,d4) + k;

 

% edge traction -> nodal loads, text (3.47)

F([6 12 18]) = [-2500 -5000 -2500];

% fixed edge, then solve

dof_k = [1 2 7 8 13 14];

dof_u = setdiff(1:ndof, dof_k);

U = zeros(ndof,1);

U(dof_u) = K(dof_u,dof_u) \ F(dof_u);

Run it: U(dof_u) reproduces (3.49). Text §3.4.1 is the full listing; §3.4.2 is the 8-node version with 16 × 16 blocks.
