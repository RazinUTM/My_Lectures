Block 1 · the post-processing formula

Two matrix products: [B] gives strains, [E] gives stresses

Step 1 · locate

Find the element, convert to local coordinates (the local-coordinate rule from heat transfer), take its eight nodal displacements in local order.

Step 2 · strains

Evaluate [B] at the point (3 × 8) and multiply by {û}ᵀ (8 × 1): three strains.

Step 3 · stresses

Multiply by [E] (3 × 3): σxx, σyy, σxy. Then principal and von Mises if the design needs them.

Why

Software does exactly this at the Gauss points of every element and then averages to the nodes for the contour plot. Hand calculation lets you see what is averaged.

Activity 1 · solution

Tension above the neutral axis; σyy small, as beam theory assumes

Why

σxx > 0 above mid-height: top fibre in tension. σxy carries the end shear; σyy is small.

Watch out

Keep six decimals: v6 and v9 differ in the fourth. With three decimals εyy doubles and σyy becomes −214.

Block 1 · principal and von Mises stresses

From three components to the two numbers a design check uses

Principal stresses

The largest tension (σ1) governs cracking in concrete; the direction is at ½·atan(2σxy/(σxx − σyy)) from the x-axis.

Von Mises

The yield check for steel: σvM against fy. ANSYS plots it by default — know what it is before you read the colour.

Block 2 · stresses on a fine mesh

At element centroids, away from the support, a 32 × 16 mesh matches beam theory

Reading the plot

  • Linear through the depth; zero at mid-height: beam theory holds away from the ends.
  • Largest at the fixed end, zero at the free end: M = P(L − x).
  • Each element has one centroid value; the contour is piecewise constant.

Watch out

Beam theory at the corner tends to 6 000; the FE value passes it and keeps rising with every refinement. The elastic solution is infinite there — refining chases infinity.

Design practice: do not read a peak at a modelled corner. Report stresses a short distance away (St-Venant), or model the support realistically, or use a stress linearisation.

Block 2 · a concentration is not a singularity

A hole concentrates stress, and the peak converges

Image: PyMAPDL documentation, “MAPDL 2D plane stress concentration analysis” (PLANE183, plane stress with thickness), © ANSYS, Inc., MIT licence.

The MAPDL example

  • Steel plate 0.4 m × 0.1 m in tension, hole d/W = 0.3, 50 elements around the hole.
  • Far field 10.0 MPa; peak von Mises 33.4 MPa at the edge of the hole.
  • On the net section Kt = 2.34; Peterson’s formula gives 2.35.

Check

A smooth hole has a finite peak: refine and it converges, so it can be reported once checked — here against Peterson.

Watch out

The fixed corner has no finite peak: it grows with every refinement (previous slide). Same plots, opposite verdicts.

The 8-node element

Mid-side nodes, quadratic edges, 16 dofs: fewer elements for the same accuracy in bending. Text Fig. 3.6, (3.50)–(3.53), Table 3.2.

SEAA 4243 · Plane Stress — Part 3 · Project

Block 3 · the element · text Fig. 3.3, Fig. 3.6

Two elements, 13 nodes, 26 dofs for the whole cantilever

Shape functions of the text's (2.31): corner nodes and mid-side nodes have different forms.

Why

Quadratic along each edge: the element can curve, so bending no longer forces spurious shear. That is the whole advantage.

Block 3 · edge loads on a three-node edge · text (3.51)

One sixth to each corner, two thirds to the mid-side node

Why

Not half-and-half any more: the quadratic edge shape functions integrate to b/6, 2b/3, b/6. The total is still qyb.

Block 4 · four things every FE results section needs

Idealisation, convergence, an independent check, quantified conclusions

1 · Idealisation stated

Plane stress or plane strain and why; thickness; material constants with units; supports and loads with a sketch; what was left out (self-weight? openings? reinforcement?).

2 · Convergence table

At least three meshes; the quantity of interest for each; the change between the last two as a percentage. One table, one sentence saying which mesh the results come from.

3 · One independent check

Beam theory, equilibrium of reactions against applied loads, a textbook stress-concentration factor, or a hand calculation on a coarse mesh. Report the difference with its sign.

4 · Quantified conclusions

“The tip deflection is 0.191 m, within 0.7% of beam theory” — not “the results agree well”. Say where the maximum is, what it means for the design, and what the model cannot tell you.

Block 4 · the cantilever written up in five sentences

A model paragraph for the project report

The cantilever (4 m × 2 m, t = 0.01 m, E = 200 × 10⁶ N m⁻², ν = 0.33) was idealised as plane stress with the left edge fully fixed and the 5 kN m⁻¹ end load applied as an edge traction. Four-node meshes of 2 × 2, 8 × 4, 16 × 8 and 32 × 16 elements gave tip deflections of 0.131, 0.182, 0.189 and 0.191 m; the change between the last two meshes is 1.0%, so the 32 × 16 results are reported. Timoshenko beam theory gives 0.192 m (0.160 m bending, 0.032 m shear), 0.7% above the FE value. The bending stress at element centroids at mid-span agrees with M(y − 1)/I to within 0.25%; the stress at the fixed corner increases without bound under refinement and is not reported. The reactions sum to 10.0 kN upward and 40.0 kN m, equal to the applied load and its moment.

Check

Every sentence carries a number, a comparison or a decision. No adjectives.

Why

Five sentences, one table, two figures: that is the whole results section of a good project report.

Block 5 · candidate structures

Four families that fit the course; propose your own if it is 2D and checkable

Shear wall with openings

Text Exercise 3.4: 5 m × 6 m, t = 0.2 m, 125 kN m⁻¹ on top, two openings. Plane stress. Check: cantilever beam + stress concentration at opening corners.

Deep beam or transfer girder

Span-to-depth about 2; point or distributed load; supports on bearing pads. Plane stress. Check: beam theory for deflection, strut-and-tie for the stress path.

Gusset or connection plate

Steel plate with bolt-hole or weld loads; t = 10–20 mm. Plane stress. Check: net-section stress, stress-concentration factor at the hole (≈ 3).

Dam or retaining wall section

Per metre run with hydrostatic pressure and self-weight. Plane strain. Check: overturning and base stress from statics.

Watch out

Choose something with a known hand check. A structure you cannot check is a structure you cannot defend in the presentation.

Block 5 · two modelling tips for the project

Use symmetry, and do not read stresses under a point load

Indirect tensile (Brazilian) test

  • A 150 mm disk squeezed across its diameter by two loads P; plane strain.
  • Two lines of symmetry → model one quarter: rollers on the cut edges (no displacement normal to them).
  • The load is a point force: the stress under it is singular. Read results away from it, or spread it over the real contact width.
