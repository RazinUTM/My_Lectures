The text’s cantilever plate (§3.4): 4 m × 2 m, t = 0.01 m, E = 200 MPa, ν = 0.33, 5 kN/m down on the free end. Change the element, the mesh and the idealisation, and watch the tip deflection.

Your tasks

  1. 4-node elements, 2 × 2 mesh: read the tip deflection. How far is it from beam theory?
    Check answer

    −0.1306 m, the text’s (3.49). Timoshenko beam theory gives −0.192 m (0.160 bending + 0.032 shear), so the hand mesh is 32% too stiff.

  2. Same eight elements, two layouts: 4-node 4 × 2 and 8 × 1. Then try 2 × 4. Which is stiffest, and why?
    Check answer

    4 × 2: −0.164 m; 8 × 1: −0.166 m; 2 × 4: −0.133 m. Elements that are long in the bending direction (a/b large) cannot bend without a spurious shear strain, so they lock. Element shape matters more than element count.

  3. Find the coarsest mesh within 2% of −0.192 m, first with 4-node and then with 8-node elements. Compare their degrees of freedom.
    Check answer

    4-node 16 × 8: −0.1886 m (−1.7%) with 306 dofs. 8-node 4 × 2: −0.1885 m (−1.8%) with 74 dofs. The quadratic element needs about a quarter of the unknowns because its edges can curve.

  4. Switch to plane strain on a fine mesh. What is the ratio of the two deflections, and why?
    Check answer

    About 0.90 (−0.171 against −0.191 m on 32 × 16). Plane strain stops the plate thinning, so the bending stiffness rises from E to E/(1 − ν²); 1 − ν² = 0.89.

More to try (the ANSYS demo’s wrong models)
  • Load: point force at the top corner. The loaded node deflects 20% more than its neighbours; mid-span is unchanged.
  • Support: vertical only. Nothing stops the plate sliding in x, and the solver refuses.
  • Model data: thickness 1 m instead of 0.01 m. The deflection falls by exactly 100.

Both element types, five meshes each, with the current idealisation, load, support and model data.

Rectangular 4-node (2 × 2 Gauss points) and 8-node serendipity (3 × 3) elements; [k] = t∫[B]ᵀ[E][B] dA, text (3.42); edge traction lumped consistently (qb/2, or b/6, 2b/3, b/6); fixed dofs eliminated; banded Cholesky. The 4-node element reproduces the text’s closed-form stiffness exactly. Timoshenko reference: PL³/(3EI) + PL/(κGA) with κ = 5/6.
