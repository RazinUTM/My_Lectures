Block 1 · when is 2D enough?

The same physics in 3D and in 2D

3D: a clamped beam sagging under its own weight (FEniCS). H. P. Langtangen and A. Logg, Solving PDEs in Python (Springer, 2016), CC BY 4.0.

2D: a spanner in plane stress, displacement and von Mises stress. SolidsPy (J. Gómez and N. Guarín-Zapata), MIT licence.

Why

3D: three displacements u, v, w and six stresses at every point.

Check

2D plane stress: u and v, three stresses, σzz = 0 because both faces are free. Valid when the body is thin and the loads act in its plane.

Ask the class which of the two could be modelled in 2D. The spanner: yes, thin and loaded in its plane (plane stress). The beam: a slender bar is better modelled with beam theory, or in 2D only if it is a thin plate loaded in its plane. Our cantilever plate (4 m × 2 m × 10 mm) is the spanner case.
