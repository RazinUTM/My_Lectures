Every sub-frame is a cut: what does each keep?

One-level

the beams of one floor + the columns above and below

full K; every joint free

beams and columns together

Two-free-joint

one beam + its two columns + its neighbours

the beam full K; neighbours ½K, fixed

one beam at a time

the beams on knife edges; then each joint

¾K end spans; ½K in the column sub-frame

Unit 3: beams, then columns

Block C · talk 5 · 5 minutes

Which values go into the design

Beams

The envelope of the method you used: both faces of every interior support, the sagging, the least span moment, the shears.

End support

A sub-frame: its envelope value (104.84 at A). The continuous beam: Mc,u + Mc,l (120.06). Never below 0.15Mspan.

Columns

The larger of the one-free-joint and one-level values (30.29 against 24.25 above B); ½ at a fixed far end; check with the largest and the smallest axial force.

Use one model throughout: the small span moment of one model with the small support moment of another is unsafe.

Workbook 4.7

Professional Habit 4

Know which model you are in

Continuous beam

¾K only for an end span with a pinned far end; M = 0 at the pin.

One-free-joint

½K for both beams; one joint, one balance; the columns only.

One-level

Full K; every joint balanced every row; the columns in ΣK.

Two-free-joint

Full K for the beam, ½K for its neighbours; nothing carried to the fixed ends.

In every model: Mspan = VL²/2w − ML whenever the end hogs, and ΣM = 0 at every free joint.
