# Unit 5 Frame Analysis III
SEAA 4333 Reinforced Concrete Design II, Week 5. Units 3 and 4 analysed a braced frame under vertical load. An unbraced frame must also carry the wind. This Unit finds how much: the design wind speed of MS 1553:2002 at each level of a building, the pressure on the windward and leeward walls, and the force that one frame collects at each floor. Which face of the building the wind blows on decides which frames carry it, and the same building can give two answers that differ by a quarter. Notes, slides, model answers, exercises with new numbers on every attempt, and the Wind Lab.

[start-outcomes] After this part you should be able to
1. say whether a frame is braced or unbraced, and which frames of a building carry the wind from each direction, with the breadth b and the depth d of MS 1553;
2. find the design wind speed at each level of a building by MS 1553:2002, from V_s , M_{z,cat} , M_s , M_h and the importance factor;
3. find the external pressure coefficients of both walls and the dynamic response factor, and the net design pressure at each level;
4. turn the pressures into the wind force that one frame carries at each floor, and check the minimum design wind load;
5. use the simplified procedure of Appendix A for a low-rise building, and add the EC2 geometric imperfections as a second lateral load.

[notes-n1] Notes 1. Unbraced frames and the lateral load
Units 3 and 4 analysed a braced frame. The wind on the building went to shear walls or a core, and the frame carried the vertical load alone. Without that bracing the frame itself must resist the wind, and the beams and columns take moments from it at every floor. This Unit finds the wind; Unit 6 analyses the frame under it.
Braced and unbraced frames A braced frame does not contribute to the overall stability of the building: the lateral actions go to bracing members (shear walls, a lift or stair core), and the frame supports the vertical actions only. An unbraced frame contributes to the stability: all lateral actions are carried by its beams and columns, which support both the vertical and the lateral actions (course notes, Types of Frame).
[Figure: Figure 1. A frame carries a horizontal force only through rigid joints or fixed bases (b); with pinned joints it needs bracing (c).]
A frame with pinned beam-to-column connections and pinned bases is not stable under a lateral force: it folds sideways like a parallelogram (Figure 1a). It becomes stable in two ways. Rigid joints, or fixed bases, let the frame resist the sway by bending its members (b): this is the unbraced frame of this Unit. Or a stiff core or wall holds the frame up, and the frame can then have pinned joints (c).
Two lateral actions act on an unbraced frame. The first is the wind , from MS 1553:2002, the subject of §2 to §5. The second comes from the building itself: no frame is built exactly plumb, and EC2 5.2 asks for the effect of a small out-of-plumb, applied as horizontal forces at each floor (§6).
How the wind reaches a frame. The wind presses on the walls. The walls span between the floors, so each floor slab collects the pressure on half a storey above and half a storey below. The slab is very stiff in its own plane and passes that force to the frames, each frame taking the share of its own width. A plane frame therefore receives one horizontal force at each floor level, and the analysis of Unit 6 starts from those forces.
Quick check (not graded): A building has rigid beam-to-column joints and no shear walls or core. Which statement is right?
Options: Its frames are braced and carry vertical load only | The slabs carry the wind to the foundation on their own | Its frames are unbraced and must carry the wind as well | The wind can be ignored below 25 m
Explanation shown after answering: With no bracing, the frames give the building its stability: they are unbraced and carry the vertical and the lateral actions. The slabs only collect the wind and share it between the frames.
Key points:
- Braced: the bracing takes the lateral load. Unbraced: the beams and columns take it, by bending at rigid joints.
- Two lateral actions: the wind (MS 1553) and the geometric imperfections (EC2 5.2).
- The floors collect the wind and give each frame one force per floor, in proportion to its width.

[notes-n2] Notes 2. Wind on a building, and the procedures of MS 1553
Wind flowing round a building pushes on the face it meets and pulls on the others. On the windward wall the air slows down against the building and the pressure rises (+). It separates at the corners, so the side walls, the roof and the leeward wall at the back are in suction (−). For the frame, the windward push and the leeward pull act in the same direction: both move the building downwind.
The intensity of the wind action grows with the square of the wind speed, and the speed depends on the site: its region, the height above ground, the topography and the roughness of the terrain around it. A building answers the gusts in two parts: a background part, the static deflection under the pressure, and a resonant part, a vibration that matters only for flexible buildings. MS 1553 treats both with a static pressure, raised by a dynamic response factor when the building is flexible (§4).
Procedure of MS 1553:2002 Use it for Pressure
Simplified , Appendix A rectangular buildings up to 15 m high, h/b_{min} ≤ 3, importance categories I and II, not on an exposed site 0.613 V_s² M_{z,cat}² (C_{p,e} − C_{p,i})
Analytical , Section 2 rectangular buildings up to 200 m high and roof spans up to 100 m 0.613 V_{des}² C_{fig} C_{dyn}
Wind tunnel complex shapes and very tall or flexible structures from the model test
This Unit follows the analytical procedure for the course building of 25 m (§3 to §5) and the simplified procedure for a low-rise building (§6). Both lead to the same chain of quantities:
basic wind speed V_s → site wind speed V_{sit} → design wind speed V_{des} at each level → pressure on each wall → net pressure at each level → force W_k on the frame at each floor.
Quick check (not graded): A three-storey office, 11 m high and rectangular on plan, stands in a suburb. Which procedure of MS 1553 may be used for its wind load?
Options: Only the wind tunnel procedure | Only Appendix A | Only the analytical procedure | Appendix A or the analytical procedure
Explanation shown after answering: It meets the limits of Appendix A (h ≤ 15 m, rectangular, an office is category II), and the analytical procedure covers any rectangular building up to 200 m. Both are allowed; §6 compares them for such a building.
Key points:
- Windward pressure and leeward suction both push the frame downwind; the net pressure is their difference p_W − p_L.
- Appendix A for low-rise rectangular buildings; the analytical procedure up to 200 m; the wind tunnel for the rest.
- The chain: V_s → V_{sit} → V_{des} → p → W_k at each floor.

[notes-n3] Notes 3. The design wind speed
The basic wind speed V_s is read from the map of MS 1553 (Figure A1): 33.5 m/s in Zone I and 32.5 m/s in Zone II; Kuala Lumpur is in Zone I. The site wind speed adjusts it to the site and the height, and the design wind speed adds the importance of the building:
EQUATION (5.1): V_{sit} = V_s\,M_d\,M_{z,cat}\,M_s\,M_h
EQUATION (5.2): V_{des} = V_{sit}\;I \qquad\qquad V_{des,z} = V_{des}\,\dfrac{M_{z,cat}(z)}{M_{z,cat}(h)}
M_d = 1.0 is the wind directional multiplier, the same for every direction in MS 1553. M_s = 1.0 when shielding by other buildings is ignored, and M_h = 1.0 on level ground; both rise or fall only for particular sites (Table 4.3, Section 4.4). The multiplier that changes most is M_{z,cat} , the terrain/height multiplier of Table 4.1: the wind is slowed near the ground, and more so over rough terrain.
Terrain category Surroundings (MS 1553 4.2.1, in brief) M_{z,cat} at 5 m at 25 m
1 exposed open terrain with few or no obstructions, open water 1.05 1.205
2 open terrain with well-scattered obstructions, such as farmland 0.91 1.100
3 many closely spaced obstructions of house size: suburbs 0.75 0.970
4 many large, high, closely spaced obstructions: city centres, industrial complexes 0.75 0.775
[Figure: Figure 2. M_{z,cat} grows with height and falls with the roughness of the terrain. Between the tabulated heights it is interpolated linearly.]
For the notes’ building (terrain category 3, roof at h = 25 m), M_{z,cat} lies between 0.94 at 20 m and 1.00 at 30 m: 0.970. With V_s = 33.5 m/s, V_{sit} = 33.5 × 1.0 × 0.970 × 1.0 × 1.0 = 32.49 m/s.
The importance factor I of Table 3.2 raises the speed for buildings whose failure would cost more lives. Note the two different I’s of MS 1553: the importance factor I here, and the turbulence intensity I_h in §4.
Importance category (Table 3.2) Buildings I
I low hazard to human life if it fails: agricultural, temporary, minor storage 0.87
II all buildings except those of categories I, III and IV: offices, flats, shops 1.00
III more than 300 people congregating in one area 1.15
IV essential buildings and structures 1.15
The notes take category III for their building (I = 1.15), so V_{des} = 32.49 × 1.15 = 37.37 m/s at the roof. An ordinary office or block of flats is category II, I = 1.0: check the category from the use of the building, not from the example.
The speed at each level. The windward pressure changes with height, so the speed is needed at every floor. Scaling V_{des} by the ratio of the multipliers (5.2) gives V_{des,z}: at level 1 (z = 4 m), M_{z,cat} = 0.750 and V_{des,z} = 37.37 × 0.750/0.970 = 28.89 m/s. The course notes compute each level directly as V_sM_{z,cat}(z) I, which is the same number.
Quick check (not graded): Terrain category 3: M_{z,cat} = 0.75 at 5 m and 0.83 at 10 m. What is M_{z,cat} at level 2, z = 7.5 m?
Options: 0.75 | 0.79 | 0.83 | 0.80
Explanation shown after answering: Halfway between 5 and 10 m: 0.75 + 0.5 × (0.83 − 0.75) = 0.79. Table 4.1 is interpolated linearly; reading the lower row (0.75) would understate the speed at that level.
Quick check (not graded): A school hall where 400 pupils gather is designed for wind. Which importance factor I applies?
Options: 0.87 | 1.00 | 1.15 | 1.30
Explanation shown after answering: More than 300 people congregate in one area: importance category III, I = 1.15 (Table 3.2). It raises the pressure by 1.15² = 1.32.
Key points:
- V_{sit} = V_sM_dM_{z,cat}M_sM_h; V_{des} = V_{sit}I.
- M_{z,cat} from Table 4.1 by terrain category and height, interpolated; it changes most.
- I from the use of the building (Table 3.2): 1.0 for offices and flats, 1.15 where more than 300 people gather.

[notes-n4] Notes 4. From speed to pressure
The design pressure on a wall follows from the dynamic pressure of the design wind speed, 0.613V_{des}² in Pa (that is ½ρV² with ρ = 1.225 kg/m³), times two factors:
EQUATION (5.3), (5.4): p = 0.613\,V_{des}^{2}\,C_{fig}\,C_{dyn}\quad(\mathrm{Pa}) \qquad\qquad C_{fig} = C_{p,e}\,K_a\,K_c\,K_l\,K_p
The aerodynamic shape factor C_{fig} is the external pressure coefficient C_{p,e} times four factors that are 1.0 for the walls of a frame: area reduction K_a, combination K_c, local pressure K_l (for cladding near edges) and porous cladding K_p. So C_{fig} = C_{p,e}.
Windward wall (Table 5.2(a)): C_{p,e} = 0.8 with the speed varying with height, V_{des,z} at each level. (For h ≤ 25 m MS 1553 also allows 0.7 with the speed at z = h for every level.) Leeward wall (Table 5.2(b)): the suction depends on the shape of the building on plan, through d/b, and the speed is taken at z = h for every level (5.4.1), so the leeward suction is the same all the way up.
d/b (roof pitch α < 10°) ≤ 1 2 ≥ 4
C_{p,e} of the leeward wall, Table 5.2(b) −0.5 −0.3 −0.2
b and d: which face? MS 1553 (Figure 5.2) names the plan dimensions by the wind: b is the breadth of the building normal to the wind, the face it blows on, and d is the depth along the wind. A plane frame carries the wind that blows in its own plane. Frame ABCD of the course building spans the 18 m width, so it carries the wind that blows across the building, onto the 51 m face: b = 51 m, d = 18 m, d/b = 0.35, C_{p,e} = −0.5.
[Figure: Figure 3. Each wind direction has its own b and d, and its own frames.]
The course notes (Example 5) take b = 18 m and d = 51 m, the wind on the 18 m face, and then apply its pressures to frame ABCD. That wind blows along the building and is carried by the longitudinal frames. The course follows decision D57 of the RC Design app (18 Nov 2026): frame ABCD takes the wind on the 51 m face. Over the frame the difference is +28.1 %: ΣW_k = 133.9 kN against the notes’ 104.5 kN (§5).
The dynamic response factor C_{dyn} allows for the resonant part of the response. It depends on the first natural frequency n_a of the building (MS 1553 6.1): n_a > 1 Hz, the building is not wind-sensitive and C_{dyn} = 1.0; 0.2 Hz ≤ n_a ≤ 1 Hz, C_{dyn} comes from 6.2.1; below 0.2 Hz the building is outside MS 1553.
EQUATION (5.5): C_{dyn} = \dfrac{1 + 2\,I_h\sqrt{g_v^{2}\,B_s + g_R^{2}\,S\,E_t/\zeta}}{1 + 2\,g_v\,I_h}
I_h is the turbulence intensity at z = h (Table 6.1), g_v = 3.7 the peak factor of the upwind velocity, B_s the background factor, S the size reduction factor, E_t the spectrum of turbulence, g_R = √(2 ln(600n_a)) the resonant peak factor and ζ the damping ratio (0.05 for concrete at the ultimate limit state, Table 6.2). For the course building, with n_a = 0.2 Hz and b = 51 m: I_h = 0.205, B_s = 0.743, S = 0.188, E_t = 0.623, g_R = 3.094, so C_{dyn} = 1.327. Worked Example 5.1 shows each term.
n_a = 0.2 Hz is the value of the notes’ example, and exactly the lowest frequency 6.1 covers. A 25 m concrete frame is usually much stiffer, near 1.8 Hz by the common estimate 46/h; then C_{dyn} = 1.0 and every pressure falls by a quarter (Activity 5B). Take n_a from the brief or from an analysis, never from habit.
The pressures. At the roof, p_W = 0.613 × 37.37² × 0.8 × 1.327 = 0.909 kN/m², and the leeward suction p_L = 0.613 × 37.37² × (−0.5) × 1.327 = −0.568 kN/m² at every level. The net pressure on the building, p_W − p_L, is 1.477 kN/m² at the roof and 1.111 at level 1 (Figure 4).
[Figure: Figure 4. The windward pressure grows with height; the leeward suction is constant; both push the frame downwind.]
Quick check (not graded): A building is 36 m long and 15 m wide; its frames span the 15 m width. What is d/b for the wind those frames carry, and the leeward C_{p,e}?
Options: 15/36 = 0.42; −0.5 | 36/15 = 2.4; −0.28 | 15/36 = 0.42; −0.3 | 36/15 = 2.4; −0.5
Explanation shown after answering: Those frames carry the wind blowing across the building, onto the 36 m face: b = 36, d = 15, d/b = 0.42 ≤ 1, so C_{p,e} = −0.5. Swapping b and d (2.4) gives −0.28 and understates the suction by 44 %.
Quick check (not graded): A building has a first natural frequency n_a = 1.8 Hz. What is C_{dyn}?
Options: From 6.2.1, as for the course building | 1.0 | Outside MS 1553 | 0.8
Explanation shown after answering: n_a > 1 Hz: the building is not wind-sensitive and C_{dyn} = 1.0 (6.1). Section 6.2.1 is needed only between 0.2 and 1 Hz.
Key points:
- p = 0.613V_{des}²C_{fig}C_{dyn}; for walls C_{fig} = C_{p,e}.
- Windward 0.8 with V_{des,z}; leeward from d/b with V_{des} at h, the same at every level.
- b is normal to the wind, d along it; a plane frame carries the wind in its own plane.
- C_{dyn} = 1.0 when n_a > 1 Hz; Section 6.2.1 between 0.2 and 1 Hz.

[notes-n5] Notes 5. From pressure to the forces on one frame
Each floor collects the net pressure on a strip of wall: as wide as the frame’s share of the building, and as high as half the storey above plus half the storey below.
EQUATION (5.6): W_k = \left(p_W - p_L\right)\,B_w\,h_{trib}
B_w is half the distance to the next frame on each side. Frame ABCD stands between bays of 4 m and 5 m, so B_w = 2.0 + 2.5 = 4.5 m. h_{trib} is half a storey at the roof (1.75 m), a full storey of 3.5 m at levels 2 to 6, and at level 1 half of each of the two different storeys, (4.0 + 3.5)/2 = 3.75 m. The bottom half of the ground storey goes straight into the foundation and never reaches the frame’s beams.
[Figure: Figure 5. The strip of wall that each floor of frame ABCD collects.]

At level 1, W_k = 1.111 × 4.5 × 3.75 = 18.75 kN; at the roof 1.477 × 4.5 × 1.75 = 11.63 kN. The roof force is the smallest even though the pressure is largest there, because it collects only half a storey.
The minimum wind load (2.4.2). The wind load on the main structure shall not be less than 0.65 kN/m² times the area projected on a vertical plane normal to the wind. Average the net pressure over the height, the bottom half of the ground storey included: here p_{avg} = 1.279 kN/m² ≥ 0.65, so the minimum does not govern. When it does, every level force is scaled by 0.65/p_{avg} (§6). Frictional drag (2.5.3) is added only when d/h or d/b exceeds 4; here they are 0.72 and 0.35.
Characteristic and design values. W_k is a characteristic action. The course method of the notes (Examples 6 and 7) takes BS 8110 load combination 3: W_d = 1.2W_k, with 1.2G_k + 1.2Q_k on every span. EN 1990 Eq. 6.10 uses 1.5W_k when the wind leads and 1.5 × 0.5 = 0.75W_k when the imposed load leads (ψ_0 = 0.5 for wind). Unit 6 forms these combinations.
Quick check (not graded): Level 1 of a frame: the storey below is 4.0 m, the storey above 3.5 m, B_w = 4.5 m and p_W − p_L = 1.111 kN/m². What is W_k?
Options: 17.50 kN | 20.00 kN | 8.75 kN | 18.75 kN
Explanation shown after answering: h_{trib} = (4.0 + 3.5)/2 = 3.75 m, so W_k = 1.111 × 4.5 × 3.75 = 18.75 kN. 17.50 takes a 3.5 m storey and 20.00 a full 4.0 m one.
Key points:
- W_k = (p_W − p_L) B_w h_{trib}: half the bays either side, half the storeys above and below.
- The roof takes half a storey; level 1 half of two different storeys; the bottom of the ground storey goes to the foundation.
- Check p_{avg} ≥ 0.65 kN/m² (2.4.2); otherwise scale every force by 0.65/p_{avg}.
- W_d = 1.2W_k in the course method (BS 8110 combination 3); 1.5W_k or 0.75W_k by EN 1990.

[notes-n6] Notes 6. Low-rise buildings, and the second lateral load
Appendix A simplifies the procedure for rectangular buildings up to 15 m high. There is no importance factor, no directional, shielding or hill multiplier and no C_{dyn}; M_{z,cat} comes from Table A1 and the leeward coefficient from Table A2 (the same values as Tables 4.1 and 5.2(b) at these heights):
EQUATION (5.7): p = 0.613\,V_s^{2}\,M_{z,cat}^{2}\,\left(C_{p,e} - C_{p,i}\right)\quad(\mathrm{Pa})
The internal pressure coefficient C_{p,i} is +0.6 or −0.3 for an enclosed building, both to be considered for the walls and the cladding. On the frame it cancels: the same internal pressure pushes out on the inside of the windward wall and the inside of the leeward wall, so the net horizontal force is 0.613V_s²M_{z,cat}² (C_{p,e,W} − C_{p,e,L}).
For a three-storey building on the course plan (h = 11 m, terrain category 3, wind on the 51 m face), Faded Example 5.2 finds net pressures of 0.634, 0.587 and 0.553 kN/m² at the roof, level 2 and level 1. Their average over the height is p_{avg} = 0.577 kN/m², below 0.65: the minimum of A2.2 governs and every force is multiplied by 0.65/0.577 = 1.126. On low buildings in rough terrain the minimum often decides the wind load.
The second lateral load: geometric imperfections (EC2 5.2). No frame is built exactly plumb. EC2 treats the frame as leaning by a small angle θ_i and applies, at each floor, a horizontal force equal to that angle times the vertical load that enters the frame there:
EQUATION (5.8), (5.9): \theta_i = \theta_0\,\alpha_h\,\alpha_m,\quad \alpha_h = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{l}}\ \left(\tfrac{2}{3} \le \alpha_h \le 1\right),\quad \alpha_m = \sqrt{0.5\left(1 + \dfrac{1}{m}\right)} \qquad H_i = \theta_i\,N_i
θ_0 = 1/200 is the recommended basic value, l the height of the frame in metres and m the number of columns that share the load. For the course frame, l = 25 m gives α_h = 2/√25 = 0.40, raised to the lower limit 2/3; four columns give α_m = 0.791; so θ_i = (1/200)(2/3)(0.791) = 1/379. A floor that brings about 1350 kN into the frame then adds H_i ≈ 1350/379 = 3.6 kN.
Which route uses them? The RC Design app adds the imperfections to the wind on the EN 1990 route by default (decision D83, agreed 15 Nov 2026): for the course frame they raise the lateral forces by about a quarter. The course method of the notes, BS 8110 combination 3, analyses the wind alone. Unit 6 states which route each example takes.
Quick check (not graded): A frame 25 m high has four columns. What is θ_i by EC2 Eq. 5.1, with θ_0 = 1/200?
Options: 1/200 | 1/379 | 1/632 | 1/253
Explanation shown after answering: α_h = 2/√25 = 0.40 is below the limit, so α_h = 2/3; α_m = √(0.5 × 1.25) = 0.791; θ_i = 0.00264 = 1/379. 1/632 forgets the lower limit on α_h.
Key points:
- Appendix A: p = 0.613V_s²M_{z,cat}²(C_{p,e} − C_{p,i}); C_{p,i} cancels on the frame.
- On low buildings the minimum of 0.65 kN/m² often governs.
- Imperfections: θ_i = θ_0α_hα_m, H_i = θ_iN_i at each floor; with the wind on the EN 1990 route.

[examples-x3] Example 3 · Explain-type questions
Exam type short written answers Time ≈ 3 min each
Questions
- Why is the leeward suction the same at every level, while the windward pressure grows with height?
- Frame ABCD spans the 18 m width of the course building. Why does it carry the wind on the 51 m face, and not the wind on the 18 m face that the notes use?
- The internal pressure coefficient of an enclosed building is +0.6 or −0.3. Why does it not enter the wind force on the frame?
- A low building in a suburb can be designed for more wind than its pressures give. Why?
Write your four answers first, then open the model answers one by one.
## Answer 1 The suction is set by the flow behind the building
The windward wall meets the oncoming wind, whose speed grows with height, so p_W follows V_{des,z}. The leeward wall sits in the wake: the flow separates at the roof edge and the suction behind is governed by the speed at the top, so MS 1553 5.4.1 takes V_{des} at z = h for every level.
## Answer 2 A plane frame carries the wind in its own plane
A frame resists sway in its own plane only. Frame ABCD lies across the building, so it resists the wind that blows across it, onto the 51 m face (b = 51, d = 18 m, leeward −0.5). The wind on the 18 m face blows along the building and goes to the longitudinal frames. The notes’ choice gives 104.5 kN on ABCD against 133.9 kN (decision D57).
## Answer 3 It acts on both walls and cancels
The internal pressure pushes outwards (or pulls inwards) equally on the inside of the windward and the leeward wall. Its horizontal resultants are equal and opposite, so the net force on the frame is 0.613V_s²M_{z,cat}²(C_{p,e,W} − C_{p,e,L}). It still matters for the walls, the cladding and the roof.
## Answer 4 The minimum design wind load
MS 1553 sets a floor of 0.65 kN/m² on the projected area for the main structure (2.4.2, A2.2). Low in rough terrain, M_{z,cat} is near 0.75 and the net pressures fall below it: Faded Example 5.2 averages 0.577 kN/m², so every force is scaled by 1.126.

[glossary-unbraced-frame] Glossary: unbraced frame
unbraced frame: A frame that gives the building its stability: rigid joints or fixed bases let its beams and columns carry the lateral actions as well as the vertical ones.

[glossary-braced-frame] Glossary: braced frame
braced frame: A frame whose lateral actions go to bracing members (shear walls, a core); it carries the vertical actions only.

[glossary-basic-wind-speed-v-s] Glossary: basic wind speed V_s
basic wind speed V_s: The wind speed of the region from the map of MS 1553 (Figure A1): 33.5 m/s in Zone I, 32.5 m/s in Zone II.

[glossary-site-wind-speed-v-sit] Glossary: site wind speed V_{sit}
site wind speed V_{sit}: V_s M_d M_{z,cat} M_s M_h : the basic speed adjusted for direction, terrain and height, shielding and hills (Eq. 1).

[glossary-design-wind-speed-v-des] Glossary: design wind speed V_{des}
design wind speed V_{des}: V_{sit} × I, at the roof; at a lower level V_{des,z} = V_{des} M_{z,cat} (z)/M_{z,cat} (h).

[glossary-terrain-category] Glossary: terrain category
terrain category: The roughness of the ground upwind, from 1 (open water, flat open land) to 4 (city centres); it sets M_{z,cat} .

[glossary-m-z-cat] Glossary: M_{z,cat}
M_{z,cat}: The terrain/height multiplier of Table 4.1 (Table A1 in Appendix A), interpolated linearly in height.

[glossary-importance-factor-i] Glossary: importance factor I
importance factor I: Table 3.2: 0.87 (category I), 1.0 (II: offices, flats), 1.15 (III: more than 300 people in one area; IV: essential buildings). Not the turbulence intensity I_h .

[glossary-windward-leeward] Glossary: windward, leeward
windward, leeward: The wall the wind blows on (pressure, +) and the wall at the back (suction, −). Both push the frame downwind.

[glossary-b-d] Glossary: b, d
b, d: MS 1553 Figure 5.2: b is the breadth of the building normal to the wind, d its depth along the wind. A plane frame carries the wind in its own plane.

[glossary-c-p-e] Glossary: C_{p,e}
C_{p,e}: External pressure coefficient: 0.8 on the windward wall (Table 5.2(a)); on the leeward wall from d/b (Table 5.2(b)): −0.5 for d/b ≤ 1, −0.3 at 2, −0.2 from 4.

[glossary-c-fig] Glossary: C_{fig}
C_{fig}: Aerodynamic shape factor C_{p,e} K_a K_c K_l K_p ; for the walls of a frame the K factors are 1.0.

[glossary-c-dyn] Glossary: C_{dyn}
C_{dyn}: Dynamic response factor: 1.0 when n_a > 1 Hz; from 6.2.1 between 0.2 and 1 Hz (Eq. 10).

[glossary-n-a] Glossary: n_a
n_a: The first natural frequency of the building in the direction of the wind; it decides whether the building is wind-sensitive (6.1).

[glossary-net-pressure] Glossary: net pressure
net pressure: p_W − p_L at a level: the windward pressure minus the (negative) leeward suction.

[glossary-b-w-h-trib] Glossary: B_w , h_{trib}
B_w , h_{trib}: The width of building a frame carries (half the bay on each side) and the height a floor carries (half the storey above and below).

[glossary-minimum-design-wind-load] Glossary: minimum design wind load
minimum design wind load: 0.65 kN/m² times the projected area for the main structure (2.4.2, A2.2): scale the forces by 0.65/p_{avg} when the average net pressure is lower.

[glossary-c-p-i] Glossary: C_{p,i}
C_{p,i}: Internal pressure coefficient, +0.6 or −0.3 for an enclosed building; it cancels in the force on the frame.

[glossary-geometric-imperfection] Glossary: geometric imperfection
geometric imperfection: The out-of-plumb of EC2 5.2: θ_i = θ_0 α_h α_m , applied as H_i = θ_i N_i at each floor.

[glossary-professional-habit-5] Glossary: Professional Habit 5
Professional Habit 5: Draw the plan and the wind arrow before any number: mark b, d and the frames that carry this wind.
