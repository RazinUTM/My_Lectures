B, C, A, and what each means for a column

W1

B: mid-height

An unbraced column bends in double curvature, with a point of contraflexure in its height.

W2

C: distance from the centroid

Edge columns carry the largest wind axial force; it adds to NEd in the column design.

W3

A: half stiffness

Today EC2 counts each beam as 2EI/l in k: a cracked stiffness, used for buckling.

Block A · talk 1 of 2

Braced or unbraced? Ask who carries the wind

Braced: walls or a core carry the horizontal load; the column does not sway.

Unbraced: the frame bends to resist it; the column sways.

Our project frame has no walls: its columns are unbraced.

Activity 7A · reveal

C: 1.22l, about twice as long, and λlim falls too

Braced

l₀ = 0.5l × (1 + 0.1/0.55) = 0.59l

C from the end moments: up to 2.7

Unbraced

√(1 + 10 × 0.01/0.2) = 1.22

(1 + 0.1/1.1)² = 1.19

C = 0.7 whatever the moments

So

λ doubles and λlim drops: an unbraced column is almost always slender.
