# Unit 8 Columns II
SEAA 4333 Reinforced Concrete Design II, Week 9. Design a short column for axial load and moment: the design moment with imperfections and the minimum eccentricity, the N–M interaction diagram, design charts and the direct check, then the bars, cover and links. Does more axial load raise or lower a column’s moment capacity? Notes, slides, model answers, exercises with new numbers on every attempt, and the Column Lab.

[start-outcomes] After this part you should be able to
1. find the design moment of a non-slender column from its end moments, the imperfection e_i = l_0 /400 and the minimum eccentricity e_0 ;
2. calculate N and M of a section for a given neutral axis depth by strain compatibility, and sketch its N–M interaction diagram with the squash, x = h, balance and pure-bending points;
3. find the longitudinal steel from a design chart, check it against A_{s,min} and A_{s,max} , and confirm M_{Rd} ≥ M_{Ed} for the bars chosen;
4. check the cover for durability, bond and fire, detail the links of a column, and find the slips in someone else’s design.

[notes-n1] Notes 1. The design moment
Unit 7 classified the column. If it is non-slender about the axis of bending, its second-order effects may be ignored, and this Unit designs it for the axial force and the first-order moments from the analysis. Two small allowances are added on the way, because no real column is perfectly straight and no load is perfectly centred. The table places the Unit in the nine design steps of the course notes.

Geometric imperfection (EC2 5.2) The column may be out of line or out of plumb. EC2 represents this by an eccentricity e_i of the axial load; for an isolated column in a braced structure it may always be taken as l_0/400 (EC2 5.2(9)), with l_0 the effective length from Unit 7.
EQUATION (8.1): e_i = \frac{l_0}{400}
EQUATION (8.2): M_{02} = \max\left(|M_{top}|,\,|M_{bot}|\right) + N_{Ed}\,e_i
Minimum eccentricity (EC2 6.1(4)) A section under compression is designed for at least N_{Ed}e_0, with e_0 = h/30 but not less than 20 mm, where h is the depth of the section in the plane of bending.
EQUATION (8.3): e_0 = \max\left(\frac{h}{30},\;20\ \mathrm{mm}\right), \qquad M_{Ed} = \max\left(M_{02},\;N_{Ed}\,e_0\right)
Worked Example 8.1 (§4 and Examples): l_0 = 1806 mm, N_{Ed} = 2400 kN, end moments 90 and 60 kN m, h = 400 mm. Then e_i = 1806/400 = 4.5 mm, N_{Ed}e_i = 2400 × 0.0045 = 10.8 kN m and M_{02} = 90 + 10.8 = 100.8 kN m. The minimum is e_0 = max(13.3; 20) = 20 mm, N_{Ed}e_0 = 48.0 kN m, so M_{Ed} = 100.8 kN m. For an interior column with small end moments the minimum often governs: with N_{Ed} = 1500 kN, l_0 = 3.0 m, end moment 15 kN m and h = 300 mm, M_{02} = 15 + 11.3 = 26.3 kN m but N_{Ed}e_0 = 30.0 kN m.
- The imperfection moment is added to the larger end moment, whatever its sign: with symmetric bars the column is designed for the magnitude.
- e_i and e_0 are not added together. e_i is part of the design moment; e_0 is only a floor under it.
- Check more than one load case: the largest N_{Ed} with its moment, the largest moment with its N_{Ed}, and the smallest N_{Ed} with its moment. §3 shows why the last one can govern.
Key points:
- Non-slender column: design for N_{Ed} and M_{Ed} = max(M_{02}, N_{Ed}e_0).
- M_{02} = larger end moment + N_{Ed}l_0/400.
- e_0 = max(h/30, 20 mm), with h in the plane of bending.
Quick check (not graded): A braced, non-slender column 300 × 300 has l_0 = 3.2 m, N_{Ed} = 1500 kN and a larger end moment of 40 kN m. Its design moment is
Options: 40.0 kN m | 52.0 kN m | 30.0 kN m | 70.0 kN m
Explanation shown after answering: e_i = 3200/400 = 8 mm, so M_{02} = 40 + 1500 × 0.008 = 52.0 kN m; N_{Ed}e_0 = 1500 × 0.020 = 30.0 kN m is smaller, so M_{Ed} = 52.0 kN m. 40.0 leaves out the imperfection; 70.0 adds e_0 on top, which EC2 does not ask for.

[notes-n2] Notes 2. The section under N and M
A column section is analysed with the same assumptions as a beam in RCD I (EC2 6.1(2)): plane sections remain plane; a bar has the strain of the concrete around it; concrete in tension is ignored; the concrete stress is a rectangular block of 0.567f_{ck} over a depth s = 0.8x (f_{ck} ≤ 50 MPa); the steel is elastic with E_s = 200 kN/mm² up to f_{yd} = 0.87f_{yk}. Three things are new in a column.
- N is not zero. In a beam the compression equals the tension; in a column the forces add up to the axial load: N = F_c + F_{sc} − F_s.
- The neutral axis can lie below the section (x > h): the whole section is then in compression. The strain at the most compressed face can no longer reach 0.0035, so EC2 Figure 6.1 pivots the strain diagram on 0.002 at 3h/7 from that face. In pure compression the strain is 0.002 everywhere.
- Many bars do not yield. Each bar’s stress comes from its strain, σ_s = E_sε_s, capped at ±f_{yd}.
[Figure: Strain compatibility: choose a neutral axis depth x, read the strain at each bar, turn strains into stresses and forces, and add them up to one pair (N, M). Moments are taken about the centre of the section, h/2.]
EQUATION (8.4): \varepsilon(y) = 0.0035\,\frac{x-y}{x}\ \ (x \le h), \qquad \varepsilon(y) = 0.002\,\frac{x-y}{x-3h/7}\ \ (x > h)
EQUATION (8.5): F_c = 0.567 f_{ck}\,b\,s,\ \ s = \min(0.8x,\,h); \qquad \sigma_s = E_s\varepsilon_s,\ \ |\sigma_s| \le 0.87 f_{yk}
EQUATION (8.6): N = F_c + F_{sc} - F_s, \qquad M = F_c\left(\frac{h}{2}-\frac{s}{2}\right) + F_{sc}\left(\frac{h}{2}-d_2\right) + F_s\left(d-\frac{h}{2}\right)
Here y is measured from the compression face, d_2 = c_{nom} + φ_{link} + φ/2 is the depth of the compression bars and d = h − d_2 that of the tension bars; F_{sc} and F_s are the bar forces, each from its own strain (F_s becomes a compression when x is deep). As in the course charts, the concrete displaced by the bars is ignored.
Example, the section of Worked Example 8.1 (400 × 400, 4H16, d_2 = 44 mm, C30/37) with x = 219.6 mm: s = 175.7 mm and F_c = 0.567 × 30 × 400 × 175.7 = 1195.1 kN. Top bars: ε = 0.0035 × (219.6 − 44)/219.6 = 0.0028 > 0.00217, so they yield: F_{sc} = 435 × 402 = 174.9 kN. Bottom bars: ε = 0.0035 × (219.6 − 356)/219.6 = −0.00217, they just yield in tension: F_s = 174.9 kN. N = 1195.1 + 174.9 − 174.9 = 1195 kN and M = 1195.1 × (0.200 − 0.0878) + 2 × 174.9 × 0.156 = 134.1 + 54.6 = 188.6 kN m (188.64 before rounding). One x, one point (N, M).
Key points:
- Same assumptions as beams; equilibrium now gives N = F_c + F_{sc} − F_s.
- x ≤ h: 0.0035 at the face. x > h: 0.002 at 3h/7 (EC2 Figure 6.1).
- Bar stress from strain, |σ_s| ≤ 0.87f_{yk}; moments about h/2.
Quick check (not graded): A column section has d = 350 mm and B500 bars. At what neutral axis depth does the concrete reach 0.0035 just as the tension bars yield?
Options: 175 mm | 216 mm | 280 mm | 350 mm
Explanation shown after answering: Similar triangles: x/d = 0.0035/(0.0035 + 0.00217) = 0.617, so x = 0.617 × 350 = 216 mm. This is the balance point of §3. 280 mm is 0.8d, the depth of the stress block at x = d, not a neutral axis.

[notes-n3] Notes 3. The N–M interaction diagram
Squeeze a row of loose books between your hands and you can lift the whole row: the compression lets it carry its own weight in bending. Squeeze harder and harder, though, and the books at the ends start to crush. A column section behaves the same way. Repeat the calculation of §2 for every neutral axis depth, from a sliver to far below the section, and plot each pair (M, N): the closed curve is the N–M interaction diagram . Every point inside it is a safe combination; the curve itself is the capacity.
[Figure: The hook of the week: compression lets loose blocks resist bending. Does more compression always help?]
[Figure: Four strain profiles of one section give four key points of its curve. These are the four team points of Activity 8A.]

EQUATION (8.8): x_{bal} = \frac{0.0035}{0.0035 + 0.87 f_{yk}/E_s}\,d = 0.617\,d \qquad (f_{yk} = 500\ \mathrm{MPa})

The big question. Does more axial load raise or lower the moment capacity? Both, and the balance point divides them. Below C the tension bars yield first ( tension controls ): extra compression holds the section together, as the squeeze holds the books, and M_{Rd} rises, from 60.6 kN m at N = 0 to 188.6 kN m at 1195 kN. Above C the concrete crushes before the tension bars yield ( compression controls ): the axial load already uses the concrete, so every extra kilonewton leaves less for bending, down to zero at the squash load.
[Figure: Activity 8B: the same moment needs less steel as N grows towards the balance load, then more. A column below balance must therefore also be checked with its smallest axial load.]
Key points:
- The curve is the capacity; a design point (M_{Ed}, N_{Ed}) inside it is safe.
- Key points: squash N_0, x = h, balance (x = 0.617d, largest M), pure bending.
- Below balance, more N raises M_{Rd}; above balance, more N lowers it.
Quick check (not graded): On the curve of the Worked Example 8.1 section (balance at 1195 kN), N_{Ed} rises from 600 to 900 kN while M_{Ed} stays at 140 kN m. The column becomes
Options: less safe, because it carries more load | safer, because M_{Rd} rises below balance | exactly as safe: N does not affect M_{Rd} | unsafe, because it is now above balance
Explanation shown after answering: Both loads are below the balance load, where tension controls. M_{Rd} rises from 147.9 to 175.1 kN m, so the margin over 140 kN m grows from 6% to 25%. The reverse also holds: if N falls, the column below balance loses capacity.

[notes-n4] Notes 4. Design charts and the direct check
An interaction diagram belongs to one section. Divide N by bhf_{ck} and M by bh²f_{ck}, and the curves of every rectangular section with symmetric bars, f_{ck} ≤ 50 MPa and B500 steel collapse onto one family, labelled by the amount of steel ω. Only the relative bar position d_2/h remains, so there is one chart for each d_2/h.
EQUATION (8.9): \nu = \frac{N_{Ed}}{b\,h\,f_{ck}},\quad \mu = \frac{M_{Ed}}{b\,h^2 f_{ck}} \ \Rightarrow\ \omega = \frac{A_s f_{yk}}{b\,h\,f_{ck}},\qquad A_s = \omega\,\frac{b\,h\,f_{ck}}{f_{yk}}
[Figure: Design chart for d_2/h = 0.15, drawn for this course by strain compatibility (§2). The charts for 0.10 and 0.20 follow; all three are printed in the workbook.]
[Figure: Design chart for d_2/h = 0.10.]
[Figure: Design chart for d_2/h = 0.20.]
- d_2 = c_{nom} + φ_{link} + φ/2 with an assumed bar size; find d_2/h.
- Choose the chart: round d_2/h to two decimals and use that chart if there is one, otherwise the next larger d_2/h, which is on the safe side (or interpolate between two charts).
- Calculate ν and μ, find the point and read ω between the curves.
- A_s = ωbhf_{ck}/f_{yk}; apply A_{s,min} (§5) and choose bars.
- Direct check: for the bars chosen, find x with N = N_{Ed} and the M_{Rd} at that x (§2, or the Column Lab); confirm M_{Rd} ≥ M_{Ed}.
Worked Example 8.1: d_2 = 30 + 6 + 8 = 44 mm, d_2/h = 0.11, so the 0.15 chart. ν = 2400 × 10³/(400 × 400 × 30) = 0.50 and μ = 100.8 × 10⁶/(400 × 400² × 30) = 0.0525: the point lies just right of the ω = 0 curve, ω ≈ 0.08, so A_s = 0.08 × 400 × 400 × 30/500 = 768 mm². With A_{s,min} = 552 mm², 4H16 (804 mm²). Direct check with 4H16: N = 2400 kN at x = 403 mm, where M_{Rd} = 107.5 kN m ≥ 100.8 kN m.
Why the direct check? A chart read by eye is good to about ±0.02 in ω, and a misread curve is easy to miss. The original course spreadsheet read two charts a quarter to a third too low, and the bars it chose failed when checked by strain compatibility. One more line in the calculation catches that.
Key points:
- ν = N/(bhf_{ck}), μ = M/(bh²f_{ck}), with h, not d; A_s = ωbhf_{ck}/f_{yk}.
- d_2/h to two decimals: that chart, or the next larger.
- Always finish with the direct check M_{Rd} ≥ M_{Ed} for the bars chosen.
Quick check (not graded): Your column has d_2/h = 0.12 and you have charts for 0.10, 0.15 and 0.20. Without interpolating, which chart gives a safe reading closest to the truth?
Options: 0.10 | either 0.10 or 0.15, they agree | 0.15 | 0.20
Explanation shown after answering: Bars deeper in the section (larger d_2/h) have a shorter lever arm, so a chart with a larger d_2/h asks for more steel: 0.15 is safe, 0.20 is safe but further from the truth, and 0.10 would ask for too little.

[notes-n5] Notes 5. Bars, cover and fire
EQUATION (8.10): A_{s,min} = \max\left(\frac{0.10\,N_{Ed}}{0.87 f_{yk}},\;0.002\,A_c\right), \qquad A_{s,max} = 0.04\,A_c\ \ (0.08\,A_c\ \text{at laps})
- A_{s,min} (EC2 9.5.2(2)) guards against creep and shrinkage shifting the load onto the bars; for a heavily loaded column the 0.10N_{Ed}/f_{yd} term governs: Worked Example 8.1, 0.10 × 2400 × 10³/435 = 552 mm² > 0.002 × 160 000 = 320 mm².
- A_{s,max} (EC2 9.5.2(3)) keeps the concrete placeable: 4% outside laps, 8% at laps, where the bars double up.
- At least one bar in each corner, so at least four in a rectangular column (EC2 9.5.2(4)); bars at least 12 mm (RDF).
The cover is set as in RCD I, then checked for fire through the axis distance a, from the face to the centre of the main bar.
EQUATION (8.11): c_{nom} = c_{min} + \Delta c_{dev},\quad c_{min} = \max\left(c_{min,b},\,c_{min,dur},\,10\right); \qquad a = c_{nom} + \phi_{link} + \frac{\phi}{2} \ \ge\ a_{fire}
- Bond (EC2 4.4.1.2(3)): each bar needs a cover of at least its own diameter, so at the link c_{min,b} = max(φ_{link}, φ − φ_{link}).
- Durability (EC2 Table 4.4N): inside a building (XC1), 15 mm, or 10 mm with C30/37 or stronger (structural class S3). Δc_{dev} = 10 mm.
- Fire (EN 1992-1-2 Table 5.2a, method A, for braced columns within the limits of its 5.3.2): use the pair whose b_{min} does not exceed your column’s smaller side. μ_{fi}, the ratio of the axial load in fire to the resistance, may be taken as 0.7 on the safe side.

Quick check (not graded): A 300 × 300 column carries N_{Ed} = 1800 kN; the chart asks for 380 mm² of B500 steel. What area must you provide at least?
Options: 380 mm² | 414 mm² | 180 mm² | 3600 mm²
Explanation shown after answering: A_{s,min} = max(0.10 × 1800 × 10³/435; 0.002 × 90 000) = max(414; 180) = 414 mm², more than the 380 mm² the chart asks for, so 4H12 (452 mm²). 180 mm² forgets the axial-load term; 3600 mm² is A_{s,max}.

[notes-n6] Notes 6. Links and detailing
A compression bar is a thin strut. Between two links it can buckle outwards and push off the cover, so the links hold every bar in line and confine the concrete core. EC2 9.5.3 sets their size and spacing.
EQUATION (8.12): \phi_{link} \ge \max\left(6\ \mathrm{mm},\ \frac{\phi_{max}}{4}\right), \qquad s_{cl,max} = \min\left(20\,\phi_{min},\ b,\ 400\ \mathrm{mm}\right)
[Figure: Links: the spacing closes up to 0.6s_{cl,max} next to beams and slabs and at laps; every corner bar sits in a link bend, and no compression bar is more than 150 mm from a restrained one.]
- The diameter rule uses the largest bar; the spacing rule uses the smallest bar, the one most likely to buckle.
- Reduce the spacing by 0.6 within a distance equal to the larger column dimension above and below a beam or slab, and at laps when the largest bar exceeds 14 mm, with at least three links in the lap length (EC2 9.5.3(4)).
- Every corner bar is held by a link; no bar in the compression zone may be more than 150 mm from a restrained bar, otherwise add a tie (EC2 9.5.3(6)).
- Round each spacing down to a practical multiple of 25 mm, as the course notes do.
Worked Example 8.1 (4H16, 400 × 400): φ_{link} ≥ max(6; 16/4) = 6 mm, H6. s_{cl,max} = min(20 × 16; 400; 400) = 320 mm: H6-300. Within 400 mm of each floor and at the laps (16 > 14 mm): 0.6 × 320 = 192 mm, H6-175.

[examples-x3] Example 3 · Explain-type questions
Exam type short written answers Time ≈ 3 min each
Questions
- The frame analysis gives the end moments of a short column exactly. Why does EC2 still add N_{Ed}e_i to them?
- Explain, with the forces in the section, why a column below its balance point can carry more moment when its axial load increases.
- Why must a column be checked with its smallest axial load as well as its largest?
- Why does EC2 ask for closer links near beams and slabs and at laps?
Write your four answers first, then open the model answers one by one.
## Answer 1 Real columns are not straight
The analysis assumes a perfectly straight and plumb column loaded along its axis. Real columns are built within tolerances: slightly out of line, slightly out of plumb, with the load slightly off centre. The axial load then acts with an eccentricity and adds a moment the analysis never saw. EC2 5.2 represents this by e_i, which for a braced column may be taken as l_0/400; N_{Ed}e_i is added to the larger end moment. For heavily loaded columns it is not small: 10.8 kN m in Worked Example 8.1, 12% of the end moment.
## Answer 2 A deeper block with a good lever arm
Below balance the tension bars have yielded, so their force stays at f_{yd}A_s/2. From N = F_c + F_{sc} − F_s, extra axial load must then be carried mostly by a larger concrete force (the compression bars add a little as their strain grows): the neutral axis moves down and the block deepens. The block’s moment about the centre is F_c(h − s)/2 = 0.567f_{ck}b·s(h − s)/2, which grows with s as long as s < h/2. So more N gives more M, until the tension bars stop yielding at the balance point, where s = 0.8 × 0.617d ≈ 0.43h, close to the h/2 that gives the block its largest moment. Beyond it the tension bar force falls away and the block runs out of lever arm, so M_{Rd} drops.
## Answer 3 Less N can mean less capacity
Because of Answer 2: below balance, a smaller axial load gives a smaller M_{Rd}. A load case with less N but a similar moment, such as wind on a frame, uplift, or a pattern of imposed load that loads the beam but not the floors above, can therefore govern. In Faded Example 8.2 the 4H20 that carry 96.2 kN m at 350 kN carry only 80.1 kN m at 175 kN. Columns are checked for the largest N with its moment, the largest moment with its N, and the smallest N with its moment.
## Answer 4 Where the bars work hardest
The column moment is largest at its ends, next to the beams and slabs, so the bars there carry the most compression and are the most likely to buckle; the region is also disturbed by the forces entering from the floor, and the bars are often cranked or lapped there. At a lap two bars pass their force through the concrete between them, which creates splitting forces across the lap; links resist them, which is why EC2 9.5.3(4) asks for 0.6s_{cl,max} and at least three links over the lap of bars larger than 14 mm.

[glossary-axial-load-ratio] Glossary: axial load ratio ν
axial load ratio ν: N_{Ed} /(bhf_{ck} ): the vertical axis of the column design charts.

[glossary-axis-distance-a] Glossary: axis distance a
axis distance a: The distance from the concrete face to the centre of a main bar, a = c_{nom} + φ_{link} + φ/2; checked against EN 1992-1-2 Table 5.2a for fire.

[glossary-balance-point] Glossary: balance point
balance point: The state in which the concrete reaches 0.0035 just as the tension bars yield, at x = 0.617d for B500; the largest moment on the N–M curve.

[glossary-compression-controls] Glossary: compression controls
compression controls: Above the balance point: the concrete crushes before the tension bars yield, and more axial load lowers M_{Rd} .

[glossary-d-2] Glossary: d_2
d_2: The depth of the compression bars below the compression face, c_{nom} + φ_{link} + φ/2; d_2 /h selects the design chart.

[glossary-design-chart] Glossary: design chart
design chart: A family of N–M curves in the normalised axes ν and μ, one curve per steel ratio ω, for one value of d_2 /h; valid for rectangular sections with symmetric bars, f_{ck} ≤ 50 MPa and B500.

[glossary-design-moment-m-ed] Glossary: design moment M_{Ed}
design moment M_{Ed}: For a non-slender column, max(M_{02} , N_{Ed} e_0 ).

[glossary-design-yield-strain-yd] Glossary: design yield strain ε_{yd}
design yield strain ε_{yd}: f_{yd} /E_s = 435/200 000 = 0.00217 for B500: below it a bar’s stress is E_s ε.

[glossary-direct-check] Glossary: direct check
direct check: Finding M_{Rd} at N_{Ed} for the bars actually chosen, by strain compatibility, and confirming M_{Rd} ≥ M_{Ed} .

[glossary-imperfection-eccentricity-e-i] Glossary: imperfection eccentricity e_i
imperfection eccentricity e_i: The eccentricity that represents a column built out of line or out of plumb; l_0 /400 for a braced column (EC2 5.2(9)).

[glossary-lap] Glossary: lap
lap: A joint in which two bars overlap so that one passes its force to the other through the concrete; at laps of bars over 14 mm the link spacing is reduced by 0.6.

[glossary-link] Glossary: link
link: Transverse reinforcement round the main bars: it holds them against buckling and confines the core. φ ≥ max(6 mm, φ_{max} /4) (EC2 9.5.3).

[glossary-load-cases-for-a-column] Glossary: load cases for a column
load cases for a column: The largest N_{Ed} with its moment, the largest moment with its N_{Ed} , and the smallest N_{Ed} with its moment; below balance the last can govern.

[glossary-m-02] Glossary: M_{02}
M_{02}: The larger first-order end moment plus N_{Ed} e_i .

[glossary-maximum-steel-a-s-max] Glossary: maximum steel A_{s,max}
maximum steel A_{s,max}: 0.04A_c outside laps and 0.08A_c at laps (EC2 9.5.2(3)).

[glossary-mechanical-steel-ratio] Glossary: mechanical steel ratio ω
mechanical steel ratio ω: A_s f_{yk} /(bhf_{ck} ): the label of each curve on a design chart; A_s = ωbhf_{ck} /f_{yk} .

[glossary-minimum-eccentricity-e-0] Glossary: minimum eccentricity e_0
minimum eccentricity e_0: h/30 but not less than 20 mm (EC2 6.1(4)): the design moment is never less than N_{Ed} e_0 .

[glossary-minimum-steel-a-s-min] Glossary: minimum steel A_{s,min}
minimum steel A_{s,min}: max(0.10N_{Ed} /f_{yd} , 0.002A_c ) (EC2 9.5.2(2)); with at least four bars of 12 mm or more in a rectangular column.

[glossary-moment-ratio] Glossary: moment ratio μ
moment ratio μ: M_{Ed} /(bh²f_{ck} ), with the full depth h: the horizontal axis of the column design charts.

[glossary-fi] Glossary: μ_{fi}
μ_{fi}: The ratio of the axial load in a fire to the design resistance at normal temperature; 0.7 may be taken on the safe side in Table 5.2a.

[glossary-n-m-interaction-diagram] Glossary: N–M interaction diagram
N–M interaction diagram: The closed curve of the pairs (M, N) that a section can just resist, found by strain compatibility for every neutral axis depth; points inside it are safe.

[glossary-pivot] Glossary: pivot
pivot: For x > h EC2 Figure 6.1 rotates the strain diagram about a strain of 0.002 at 3h/7 from the more compressed face; in pure compression the strain is 0.002 everywhere.

[glossary-s-cl-max] Glossary: s_{cl,max}
s_{cl,max}: The largest link spacing: the least of 20 times the smallest main bar, the smaller side of the column and 400 mm (EC2 9.5.3(3)).

[glossary-squash-load-n-0] Glossary: squash load N_0
squash load N_0: The axial capacity with no moment: 0.567f_{ck} bh + 400A_s with B500 bars at a strain of 0.002.

[glossary-strain-compatibility] Glossary: strain compatibility
strain compatibility: Section analysis in which plane sections remain plane and every bar has the strain of the concrete around it: strains give stresses, stresses give forces, forces give N and M.

[glossary-tension-controls] Glossary: tension controls
tension controls: Below the balance point: the tension bars yield before the concrete crushes, and more axial load raises M_{Rd} .
