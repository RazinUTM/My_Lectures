A column on the boundary: three ways to found it

Rectangle

room to run past B: centre it on the resultant (Unit 10)

Trapezoid

length fixed at both ends: wider at the heavier column, x = (L/3)(b₁ + 2b₂)/(b₁ + b₂)

Strap

columns far apart: two footings and a beam that carries the moment; it must not bear on the soil

Block A · talk 2 · 5 minutes

The strap is a lever: moments about column B

e = LA/2 − cA/2

RA = WA + (NA·L + WR·xR)/(L − e)

RB = ΣN + ΣW − RA

BA = RA/(qall·LA)

SB = √(RB/qall)

10 % footing weights first, then the real ones and the pressures again.

Block C · talk 4 · 7 minutes

Piles: down through weak soil to firm ground

Types

precast RC or prestressed, driven; bored, cast in situ; steel; timber and bakau

Capacity Fp

a safe working load: the ultimate resistance ÷ 2 to 3, from a load test or a driving formula

A group

close friction piles: up to a third less than n single piles; end bearing on rock: about the sum

Notes §5 · Workbook 11.5

Block C · talk 5 · 5 minutes

The group: how many piles, the cap, and the load on each

n = (N + W)/Fp, rounded up

s = 3φ; side ((n − 1)·3 + 1)φ + 300

P = (N + W)/n ± M·x/Σx²

Σx² over every pile; those on the axis add zero.

Service loads against Fp. The ultimate NEd/n ± MEd·x/Σx², without the cap weight, designs the cap (Unit 12).
