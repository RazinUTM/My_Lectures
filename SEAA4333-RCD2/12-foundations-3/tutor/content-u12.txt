# Unit 12 Foundations III
SEAA 4333 Reinforced Concrete Design II, Week 13. Unit 11 sized the pile group and found the load on each pile; this Unit designs the cap that gathers them. A short, deep cap works as a truss: concrete struts from the column to each pile, held by a steel tie along the bottom. A longer cap, or one under a moment, is designed by beam theory at the column faces. Then the shear section a fifth of the pile diameter inside the piles, with the enhancement of EC2 6.2.2(6) and its limit, the column face, crack control and the detailing that anchors the tie. Why does the course notes’ cap, which passes by 1 %, fail EC2? Notes, slides, model answers, exercises with new numbers on every attempt, and the Foundation Lab.

[start-outcomes] After this part you should be able to
1. size a pile cap, its plan from the pile spacing and its depth from the guide, and choose between the truss analogy and beam theory;
2. find the tie forces of two-, three- and four-pile caps by the truss analogy, and design and place the tie bars with their anchorage;
3. find the moments at the column faces of a six-pile cap from its pile loads, and design the bars in both directions;
4. check the shear of a pile cap at the section φ/5 inside the piles, with the enhancement of EC2 6.2.2(6) and its limit, check the column face and the crack widths, and say when punching must be checked.

[notes-n1] Notes 1. The pile cap: its job, plan and depth
A pile cap gathers the column load and shares it among the piles. It is short and deep, so it does not bend like a slab: most of the load runs straight down from the column to each pile head in compression, and the bottom of the cap is pulled apart as the pile heads try to spread. Two models design it: the truss analogy for compact caps, and beam theory for longer ones.
[Figure: Pile caps of two, three, four and six piles, with s = 3φ and edges 150 mm past the pile faces (RDF §16). Rust lines: the ties of the truss analogy.]
The plan follows from Unit 11: piles at s = kφ with k = 3, and the edge of the cap 150 mm beyond the pile faces, so a square four-pile cap is (k + 1)φ + 300 each way and a six-pile cap (k + 1)φ + 300 by (2k + 1)φ + 300. The depth must give room for the anchorage of the pile bars and the column starter bars, and enough shear resistance. Mosley Table 10.5 gives a first depth for groups of up to six piles; the course notes’ 2φ + 100 gives the same values up to 550 mm piles.
Pile size φ (mm) 300 350 400 450 500 550 600 750
Cap depth, Mosley Table 10.5 (mm) 700 800 900 1000 1100 1200 1400 1800
2φ + 100, course notes (mm) 700 800 900 1000 1100 1200 1300 1600
The guide is a starting point, not a check. Shear decides the depth in the end (§4): the course notes’ four-pile cap needs 1650 mm rather than 1100 mm, and their six-pile cap of 300 mm piles needs the 1100 mm the notes give it (2φ + 500), not the table’s 700 mm, because each of its piles carries 720 kN at the ultimate limit state.
The loads that design a cap The number of piles and the service pile loads (N + W)/n ± Mx/Σx² were checked in Unit 11. The cap is designed with the ultimate pile loads N_{Ed}/n ± M_{Ed}x/Σx², without the cap’s own weight, as the course notes, Mosley’s Example 10.5 and the RC Design app do: the weight sits over the piles and adds little to the forces at the critical sections. Adding 1.35W/n to each pile is the safer variant. The cover in the notes’ examples is 75 mm, measured from the soffit; the piles stop at the bottom mat, 75 mm into the cap.

Explanation shown after answering: (3 + 1) × 450 + 300 = 2100 mm each way; Table 10.5 and 2φ + 100 both give 1000 mm. This is Mosley’s Example 10.5 and the cap of Foundation S12. The depth still has to pass the shear check of §4.
Key points:
- Plan: s = 3φ and edges 150 mm past the pile faces; four piles (k + 1)φ + 300 square.
- Depth: start from Mosley Table 10.5 (≈ 2φ + 100); shear decides.
- Design loads: the ultimate pile loads N_{Ed}/n ± M_{Ed}x/Σx², without the cap weight.

[notes-n2] Notes 2. The truss analogy
Think of the cap as a triangle. Concrete struts run from the column down to each pile head; a steel tie along the bottom joins the pile heads and stops them spreading. The column pushes the apex down, the piles push the corners up, and the tie carries the horizontal force that holds the triangle closed.
[Figure: The truss analogy for a two-pile cap (Mosley §10.7.1). The upper node sits at the centre of the column, the lower nodes where the tie crosses the pile centre lines.]
At a pile node three forces meet: the pile reaction N/2 upwards, the strut C along the line to the column, and the tie T. Their triangle is similar to the triangle of the truss, so T/(N/2) = (s/2)/d. For two piles:
EQUATION (12.1): T = \frac{N\,s}{4d} \qquad \text{(two piles; } d = h - c - \phi/2\text{)}
For three piles in a triangle the same reasoning gives the force in each side, and for four piles in a square the load is shared by two parallel trusses in each direction (Mosley Table 10.4, with 2l = s; RDF Figure 16.2):
EQUATION (12.2): T = \frac{N\,s}{9d} \qquad \text{(three piles, each side; three bands in three layers, } d = h - c - 2.5\phi\text{)}
EQUATION (12.3): T = \frac{N\,s}{8d} \qquad \text{(four piles, each truss; two trusses each way, } d = h - c - 1.5\phi\text{)}
EQUATION (12.4): A_s = \frac{T}{0.87 f_{yk}} \ \ge\ A_{s,min} = \max\!\left(0.26\frac{f_{ctm}}{f_{yk}},\ 0.0013\right) b\,d
[Figure: Four piles: two trusses each way, each carrying N/2, so the steel each way is 2T/0.87f_{yk} = Ns/(4d × 0.87f_{yk}). Where the bars may go depends on the spacing.]
A common slip with four piles is to stop at T: the bars across the cap in one direction serve both trusses of that direction, so they carry 2T. The bars go in both directions of the bottom face. With s ≤ 3φ they may be spread evenly across the width, as in all the course examples; with wider spacing only the bars within 1.5φ of a pile centre count, so they are gathered in bands over the piles. They continue past each pile and are bent up to anchor beyond the pile centre lines (§6). The effective depth follows the layers: the tie of a two-pile cap is the bottom layer, a four-pile cap uses the upper layer for both directions (on the safe side, as the notes), and the three bands of a three-pile cap lie in three layers.

Explanation shown after answering: T = 6000 × 1200/(8 × 800) = 1125 kN per truss; two trusses each way, so A_s = 2 × 1125 × 10³/435 = 5172 mm², the same as Ns/(4d × 0.87f_{yk}). 2586 mm² is one truss only: half the steel.
Key points:
- Truss: T = Ns/4d (two piles), Ns/9d each side (three), Ns/8d each truss (four, two trusses each way).
- A_s = T/0.87f_{yk}, at least A_{s,min} over the whole width; four piles need 2T each way.
- s ≤ 3φ: spread the bars evenly; s > 3φ: only bars within 1.5φ of a pile centre count.

[notes-n3] Notes 3. Beam theory
When the cap is longer, or a moment makes the pile loads unequal, treat it as a wide beam turned upside down: the piles push up, the column holds it down, and the critical section for bending is at the face of the column, as for a pad footing.
[Figure: Beam theory for a six-pile cap: each face moment comes from the piles beyond that face (app case F13, Faded Example 12.2).]
The moment at a column face is the sum, over the piles beyond that face, of each pile load times its distance to the face:
EQUATION (12.5): M = \sum_{\text{piles beyond the face}} F_i\left(x_i - \frac{c}{2}\right)
In a 2 × 3 group with the moment about y, the two end piles carry the largest load, so M_{xx} = 2F_{max}(x_{max} − c/2) designs the bars along the length. Across, one long row of three piles sums to three times the mean load whatever the moment about y, so M_{yy} = 3F_{mean}(s/2 − c/2). The larger moment takes the outer layer, d_x = h − c − φ/2; the other uses d_y = h − c − 1.5φ. Then K, z ≤ 0.95d and A_s as for a beam, with A_{s,min} over the full width, which often governs the short direction.

The two models are closer than they look. Ns/4d, the total tie force in one direction of a four-pile cap, is the moment at the column centre line, Ns/4, divided by the lever arm d; beam theory takes the smaller moment at the face, (N/2)(s/2 − c/2), with z = 0.95d. For the course notes’ four-pile cap the truss gives 7399 mm² each way and beam theory 5192 mm²: the truss is the safer model for a compact cap. Mosley keeps beam theory for caps whose span exceeds about twice their depth. In this course, caps of two, three and four piles go by the truss and six piles under a moment by beam theory, as in the notes’ two Example 5s.

Options: 1297.5 kN m | 1009.2 kN m | 980.0 kN m | 525.0 kN m
Explanation shown after answering: At the column face, 0.2 m from the centre: 2 × 720.8 × (0.9 − 0.2) = 1009.2 kN m. 1297.5 is taken at the centre line, 980.0 uses the mean load 700 kN, and 525.0 is the moment across.
Key points:
- Beam theory: M at each column face = Σ F_i(x_i − c/2) over the piles beyond it.
- 2 × 3 group: M_{xx} = 2F_{max}(x_{max} − c/2), M_{yy} = 3F_{mean}(s/2 − c/2); the larger moment in the outer layer.
- The truss uses the centre-line moment over d, so it gives more steel than beam theory for a compact cap.

[notes-n4] Notes 4. Shear: the section at φ/5 and the enhancement
A pile cap does not fail in shear like a long beam. Near the column the load reaches the piles along direct struts, which carry far more shear than the beam formula allows. EC2 lets loads close to a support be reduced by a factor β = a_v/2d before checking V_{Rd,c}, but it stops the reduction at a_v = 0.5d.
[Figure: The shear section of a pile cap, φ/5 inside the pile face (Mosley §10.7.2): V_{Ed} is the load of every pile beyond it, over the whole width when s ≤ 3φ.]
The critical section lies 20 % of the pile diameter inside the face of the pile (Mosley §10.7.2, from BS 8110). V_{Ed} is the sum of the loads of the piles beyond it: in a four-pile cap the section crosses the full width, so two piles stand beyond it, not one. The section is a_v from the column face. For a square group:
EQUATION (12.6): a_v = \frac{s}{2} - \frac{c}{2} - \frac{\phi}{2} + \frac{\phi}{5} = \frac{s}{2} - \frac{c}{2} - 0.3\,\phi
EQUATION (12.7): \beta = \frac{\max(a_v,\ 0.5d)}{2d} \le 1 \qquad \text{(EC2 6.2.2(6); bars fully anchored at the piles)}
EQUATION (12.8): \beta\,V_{Ed} \le V_{Rd,c}, \qquad V_{Ed} \le 0.5\,b\,d\,\nu f_{cd}, \quad \nu = 0.6\left(1 - \frac{f_{ck}}{250}\right)
EQUATION (12.9): V_{Rd,c} = \left[0.12\,k\,(100\rho_l f_{ck})^{1/3}\right] b\,d \ \ge\ v_{min}\,b\,d, \qquad v_{min} = 0.035\,k^{3/2}\sqrt{f_{ck}}, \quad k = 1 + \sqrt{200/d} \le 2
Three conditions come with β. It needs the main bars fully anchored at the support, here bent up beyond each pile centre line (§6). The unreduced shear must still satisfy V_{Ed} ≤ 0.5bdνf_{cd}. And for a_v < 0.5d, EC2 takes a_v = 0.5d, so β never drops below 0.25: the shear can be divided by four at most. In V_{Rd,c} the bars count in ρ_l only if they run l_{bd} + d beyond the section (EC2 Figure 6.3, the course convention from Unit 10); in every course example they do not, and v_{min} governs. With s ≤ 3φ the enhancement applies across the whole section; with wider spacing only to strips 3φ wide over the piles.
[Figure: β against a_v/d: EC2 floors it at 0.25; the course notes and Mosley do not.]
The course notes and Mosley apply β without the floor; Mosley’s own Example 10.5 has a_v = 290 mm < 0.5d = 437.5 mm and still passes with it (625 ≤ 633 kN). The course follows EC2, as the RC Design app does (the lecturer chose the conservative option on 4 October 2026). For the notes’ four-pile cap, a_v = 350 mm < 0.5d = 494 mm, so β = 0.25 and V_{Ed,red} = 0.25 × 4237.5 = 1059.4 kN against V_{Rd,c} = 760.3 kN: the 1100 mm cap fails, where the notes’ β = 0.177 gave 750.9 kN and a pass by 1 %. Inside the floor a deeper cap does not lower V_{Ed,red}; it raises V_{Rd,c}, through d more than through k. 1650 mm is the shallowest depth, in 50 mm steps, that passes; the app’s revised case F12R uses 1700 mm (Worked Example 12.1).
Quick check (not graded): The course notes’ four-pile cap: a_v = 350 mm and d = 987.5 mm, the bars bent up and anchored beyond the piles. By EC2 the factor β is
Options: 0.177 | 0.25 | 0.354 | 1.0
Explanation shown after answering: a_v = 350 < 0.5d = 494 mm, so a_v = 494 mm is used: β = 494/(2 × 987.5) = 0.25. 0.177 is the notes’ value without the floor; 1.0 would apply if the bars were not anchored at the piles.
Key points:
- Shear section φ/5 inside the pile face; V_{Ed} = all the piles beyond it, across the whole width.
- β = max(a_v, 0.5d)/2d ≥ 0.25, only with the bars anchored beyond the piles; V_{Ed} ≤ 0.5bdνf_{cd}.
- Inside the floor a deeper cap raises V_{Rd,c}, not lowers V_{Ed,red}: the notes’ 1100 mm cap needs 1650 mm.

[notes-n5] Notes 5. Punching and the column face
With piles at 3φ every pile stands inside the basic control perimeter at 2d, so the shear crosses the sections of §4 rather than a punching perimeter. What remains is the crushing check at the face of the column, and the same check round each pile.
[Figure: Close piles sit inside 2d of the column: no punching check at 2d (Mosley §10.7.3); the column face is checked instead.]
Mosley §10.7.3, the course notes and RDF §16 check punching at the control perimeters only when the piles are spaced more than 3φ apart, in the way of Unit 10 for slabs and pads. At 3φ or closer the piles lie inside the 2d perimeter and the shear is carried across the sections of §4. The face of the column is always checked (EC2 6.4.5(3)), with the mean effective depth d = h − c − φ:
EQUATION (12.10): v_{Ed,0} = \frac{\beta\,N_{Ed}}{u_0\,d} \le v_{Rd,max} = 0.4\,\nu f_{cd}, \qquad u_0 = 4c, \quad \beta = 1 + k\frac{M_{Ed}}{N_{Ed}}\frac{u_0}{W_0}
For an axial load β = 1. With a moment, W_0 = c_1²/2 + c_1c_2 at the column perimeter and k from Table 6.1 (0.6 for a square column): the six-pile cap of Faded Example 12.2 has β = 1 + 0.6 × (75/4200 m) × 1600/240000 = 1.071. The limit 0.4νf_{cd} = 4.22 N/mm² for C30/37 is the value of the 2014 amendment of EN 1992-1-1, which the course and the app use; the course notes and Mosley use 0.5νf_{cd} = 5.28 N/mm² of the 2004 text. The difference matters for the notes’ four-pile cap: 8475 × 10³/(2000 × 1000) = 4.24 N/mm², over 4.22 by 0.3 % and well under 5.28. Which value the Malaysian National Annex sets is still to be confirmed. Each pile is checked the same way, F_{max}/(u_pd) with u_p = πφ for a round pile (RDF §16); it rarely governs.
Quick check (not graded): Four 500 mm piles at 1500 mm (3φ) under a 500 mm column. Which shear checks does the course method make round the column and the piles?
Options: Punching at 2d and at every perimeter inside it, as for a pad | The column face and each pile perimeter against v_{Rd,max}; no punching perimeter, because the piles lie inside 2d | Punching at 2d only | None: the shear sections of §4 cover everything
Explanation shown after answering: At s ≤ 3φ the piles are inside the 2d perimeter, so no punching check is made there (Mosley §10.7.3); the column face and the pile perimeters are still checked for crushing.
Key points:
- s ≤ 3φ: no punching check at 2d; s > 3φ: punching as for a slab.
- Column face: βN_{Ed}/(u_0d) ≤ 0.4νf_{cd} (course; 0.5νf_{cd} in the notes), d = h − c − φ.
- Each pile: F_{max}/(πφd) against the same limit.

[notes-n6] Notes 6. Crack control and detailing
A pile cap is a deep, heavily loaded block. Most of its detailing makes sure that the tie is anchored where the struts meet it, over the piles, and that the corners and faces of the block hold together.
[Figure: Detailing a pile cap (Mosley §10.7.4, RDF §16): bent-up ties anchored beyond the pile centre lines, horizontal links, starter bars, and the top face.]
Anchorage. The main bars continue past each pile and are bent up to give a full anchorage length beyond the pile centre line: available length (edge distance − c) + (d − c) ≥ l_{bd}, with l_{bd} = (φ/4)(0.87f_{yk}/f_{bd}) = 894 mm for H25 in C30/37 (f_{bd} = 3.04 N/mm², good bond). For the notes’ cap (400 − 75) + (987.5 − 75) = 1237.5 mm ≥ 894 mm, which makes β valid. The same bars run only 1387.5 mm past the shear section, less than l_{bd} + d = 1881 mm, so they do not count in ρ_l: the two conditions differ, and both are checked.
The rest of the cage. A_{s,min} = 0.26(f_{ctm}/f_{yk})bd in both directions of the bottom face, and Mosley puts the same in the top face; EC2 9.8.1(3) lets the top and the sides stay unreinforced where no tension can develop, so the top mat is the course’s choice, not a code demand. Fully lapped horizontal links of at least H12 at no more than 250 mm run round the sides. The column starter bars are tied with links inside the cap. The piles are cut off so that they do not extend into the cap beyond the lower mat, about 75 mm, or the punching strength drops.
Cracks. As for every base: σ_s = (N_{qp}/N_{Ed})(f_{yk}/1.15)(A_{s,req}/A_{s,prov}), the spacing limit from Table 7.3N for w_k = 0.3 mm, interpolated, and the spacing provided (b − 2c − φ)/(n − 1). For the notes’ cap σ_s = 0.50 × 434.8 × 7399/7854 = 205.4 N/mm², so s_{max} = 243 mm against 142 mm provided.

Quick check (not graded): Why are the tie bars of a pile cap bent up at both ends?
Options: To resist tension in the top face | To hold the horizontal links in place | To anchor the tie beyond the pile centre lines, which also makes the β reduction valid | To keep the cover at the ends
Explanation shown after answering: The tie force is greatest at the nodes over the piles, so it must be anchored beyond them, and a straight bar has too little room (400 − 75 = 325 mm against 894 mm). EC2 6.2.2(6) allows β only with the bars fully anchored at the support.
Key points:
- Bend the ties up: (edge − c) + (d − c) ≥ l_{bd} beyond each pile centre line.
- A_{s,min} both ways, bottom (and top, Mosley); H12 horizontal links at ≤ 250 mm; starter bars with links; piles cut at the bottom mat.
- Cracks: σ_s from the quasi-permanent load and the spacing of Table 7.3N, as for every base.

[examples-x3] Example 3 · Explain-type questions
Exam type short written answers Time ≈ 3 min each
Questions
- Why does the truss analogy give more steel than beam theory for the same four-pile cap?
- In Worked Example 12.1, why does a deeper cap not reduce the reduced shear V_{Ed,red}, and how does it pass?
- When must a pile cap be checked for punching, and what is checked when it need not be?
- Mosley Table 10.5 suggests 700 mm for 300 mm piles. What happens to the cap of Faded Example 12.2 at 700 mm?
Write your four answers first, then open the model answers one by one.
## Answer 1 Centre line against face
In one direction the truss carries 2T = Ns/4d, which is the moment at the column centre line, (N/2)(s/2) = Ns/4, divided by d. Beam theory takes the moment at the column face, (N/2)(s/2 − c/2), smaller by the half-width of the column, over z = 0.95d. For the notes’ four-pile cap that is 3178 kN m over 987.5 mm against 2119 kN m over 938 mm: 7399 against 5192 mm² each way. The truss is the safer model where the struts are steep and the cap compact; beam theory suits longer caps.
## Answer 2 The floor of β
a_v = 350 mm is fixed by the plan; 0.5d is 494 mm at 1100 mm depth and grows with d, so a_v stays below 0.5d and β stays at its floor, 0.25. V_{Ed,red} = 0.25 × 4237.5 = 1059.4 kN whatever the depth. Without the floor, β = a_v/2d would keep falling and a deeper cap would seem to lower the shear, which is the reasoning EC2 rules out. The cap passes because V_{Rd,c} = v_{min}bd grows with d (v_{min} itself falls a little through k): 760 kN at 1100 mm, 1104 kN at 1700 mm.
## Answer 3 Piles outside 2d
When the piles are more than 3φ apart, some lie outside the basic control perimeter at 2d from the column, and the cap can punch as a slab: it is checked at the control perimeters as in Unit 10, and only the bars within 1.5φ of a pile centre count in the ties. At 3φ or closer the piles sit inside 2d, the shear crosses the beam sections at φ/5, and no punching perimeter is checked (Mosley §10.7.3, BS 8110 practice). The crushing checks remain: the column face, βN_{Ed}/(u_0d) ≤ 0.4νf_{cd}, and each pile perimeter.
## Answer 4 Shear sets the depth
At h = 700 mm, d_x = 617 mm. Bending needs 3979 mm² along the length (20H16 instead of 12H16), which is possible. Shear is not: a_v = 610 mm is now beyond 0.5d, so β = 610/1234 = 0.49 and V_{Ed,red} = 712.7 kN against V_{Rd,c} = 348.8 kN, a ratio of 2.0; the column face reaches 4.62 > 4.22 N/mm². 1000 mm still fails (479.5 > 468.5 kN); the notes’ 1100 mm passes. Table 10.5 suits ordinary pile loads; these 300 mm piles carry 720 kN each at the ultimate limit state, and the cap has to be deep enough for that shear.

[glossary-a-v-shear-span-of-a-cap] Glossary: a_v (shear span of a cap)
a_v (shear span of a cap): The distance from the face of the column to the shear section φ/5 inside the pile face; for a square group a_v = s/2 − c/2 − 0.3φ.

[glossary-anchorage-beyond-the-pile-centre-line] Glossary: anchorage beyond the pile centre line
anchorage beyond the pile centre line: The length a tie bar runs past the centre of a pile, straight and up its bent leg: (edge distance − c) + (d − c); it must reach l_{bd} for β to be valid.

[glossary-beam-theory] Glossary: beam theory
beam theory: Designing a pile cap as a wide inverted beam: the moment at each column face from the piles beyond it, M = ΣF_i (x_i − c/2).

[glossary-bent-up-bar] Glossary: bent-up bar
bent-up bar: A tie bar turned up at the end of the cap so that it anchors beyond the pile centre line (Mosley §10.7.4).

[glossary-shear-enhancement-factor] Glossary: β, shear enhancement factor
β, shear enhancement factor: The factor of EC2 6.2.2(6) on loads near a support: β = max(a_v , 0.5d)/2d, so 0.25 ≤ β ≤ 1; valid only with the bars fully anchored at the support.

[glossary-column-face-check] Glossary: column face check
column face check: The crushing check at the column perimeter, βN_{Ed} /(u_0 d) ≤ v_{Rd,max} (EC2 6.4.5(3)), made for every cap.

[glossary-critical-shear-section] Glossary: critical shear section
critical shear section: In a pile cap, the section a fifth of the pile diameter inside the face of the pile; V_{Ed} is the load of every pile beyond it.

[glossary-depth-guide] Glossary: depth guide
depth guide: Mosley Table 10.5: 700 mm for 300 mm piles to 1800 mm for 750 mm piles, for up to six piles; about 2φ + 100. A starting value: shear decides.

[glossary-effective-depth-by-layers] Glossary: effective depth by layers
effective depth by layers: d follows the layer of the bars: h − c − φ/2 for one layer, h − c − 1.5φ for the upper of two, h − c − 2.5φ for the top of three.

[glossary-floor-of] Glossary: floor of β
floor of β: EC2’s rule that a_v is not taken less than 0.5d, so the reduced shear is never less than a quarter of V_{Ed} ; the course notes and Mosley leave it out.

[glossary-horizontal-links] Glossary: horizontal links
horizontal links: Fully lapped links of at least H12 at no more than 250 mm round the sides of a cap, holding the corners and the bent-up bars.

[glossary-node] Glossary: node
node: A joint of the truss analogy: the upper node at the centre of the column, the lower nodes where the tie crosses the pile centre lines.

[glossary-strength-reduction-factor] Glossary: ν, strength reduction factor
ν, strength reduction factor: ν = 0.6(1 − f_{ck} /250) for cracked concrete; 0.528 for C30/37. It sets v_{Rd,max} and the limit on the unreduced shear.

[glossary-pile-cap] Glossary: pile cap
pile cap: The block that joins a group of piles to its column and shares the load among them; designed by the truss analogy or by beam theory.

[glossary-pile-cut-off-level] Glossary: pile cut-off level
pile cut-off level: The level at which a pile head is trimmed: not above the bottom mat, about 75 mm into the cap, so that it does not weaken the cap in punching.

[glossary-pile-perimeter-check] Glossary: pile perimeter check
pile perimeter check: F_{max} /(u_p d) ≤ v_{Rd,max} round each pile, u_p = πφ for a round pile (RDF §16).

[glossary-punching-in-a-pile-cap] Glossary: punching in a pile cap
punching in a pile cap: Checked at the control perimeters, as for a slab, only when the piles are more than 3φ apart and so stand outside 2d (Mosley §10.7.3).

[glossary-l-in-a-pile-cap] Glossary: ρ_l in a pile cap
ρ_l in a pile cap: The tension steel counts in V_{Rd,c} only if it runs l_{bd} + d beyond the shear section (EC2 Figure 6.3); in the course examples it does not, so v_{min} governs.

[glossary-spacing-factor-k] Glossary: spacing factor k
spacing factor k: s = kφ; the course uses k = 3. With k > 3 only the bars within 1.5φ of a pile centre count, the enhancement applies to strips 3φ wide, and punching is checked.

[glossary-starter-bars] Glossary: starter bars
starter bars: The bars left projecting from the cap to lap with the column bars, tied with links inside the cap.

[glossary-strut] Glossary: strut
strut: A band of concrete in compression carrying load from the column down to a pile head in the truss analogy.

[glossary-tie] Glossary: tie
tie: The bottom reinforcement of the truss analogy, joining the pile heads and holding them from spreading apart.

[glossary-tie-force-t] Glossary: tie force T
tie force T: Two piles Ns/4d; three piles Ns/9d each side; four piles Ns/8d each truss, two trusses each way (Mosley Table 10.4, RDF Figure 16.2).

[glossary-truss-analogy] Glossary: truss analogy
truss analogy: A model of a compact pile cap as concrete struts from the column to the piles and steel ties between the pile heads; used when the span is less than about twice the depth.

[glossary-v-rd-max] Glossary: v_{Rd,max}
v_{Rd,max}: The crushing limit at the column face: 0.4νf_{cd} = 4.22 N/mm² for C30/37 in the course (EN 1992-1-1 A1:2014); 0.5νf_{cd} = 5.28 N/mm² in the notes and Mosley.

[glossary-whole-width-enhancement] Glossary: whole-width enhancement
whole-width enhancement: With s ≤ 3φ the reduction β applies across the whole shear section; with wider spacing only to strips 3φ wide centred on the piles (Mosley §10.7.2).
