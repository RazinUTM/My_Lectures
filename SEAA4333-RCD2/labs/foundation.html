The strap footing: a seesaw

Column A stands on the site boundary and is tied by a strap to column B. The Lab takes moments about column B for the service reactions and the footing sizes, checks the pressures with the real weights, then finds the net ultimate reactions and draws the shear and moment of the strap by the course method (Notes §2–§3).

The checks, one by one

Course method of SEAA 4333 (course notes; Mosley, Bungey & Hulse §10.1–10.4, §10.6–10.7; MS EN 1992-1-1): the plan from the service loads, A = (Gk + Qk + W)/qall; the ultimate pressure pEd = NEd/A without the self-weight; e ≤ L/6: p = (N/A)(1 ± 6e/L); e > L/6: Lc = 3(L/2 − e), pmax = 2N/(BLc); bending at the column face; vertical shear at d, with ρl only for bars anchored lbd + d beyond the section (EC2 6.2.2, Fig. 6.3); punching at 2d and at every a ≤ 2d with VEd,red = NEd − pEdA(a) and vRd = vRd,c·2d/a (EC2 6.4.4(2)); the column face against 0.4νfcd (EC2 6.4.5(3), 2014 amendment). Strap footing (Mosley §10.3): moments about column B, RA = WA + (NAL + WRxR)/(L − e); ultimate reactions without the footing weights. Piles (Mosley §10.6): n = (N + W)/Fp, s = 3φ, P = (N + W)/n ± Myx/Σx² ± Mxy/Σy². Pile caps (Mosley §10.7): T = Ns/4d (two piles), Ns/8d per truss (four), M at the column faces (six); shear φ/5 inside the piles with β = max(av, 0.5d)/2d (EC2 6.2.2(6)). Units: mm, kN, m.
