Where the bars go

Bottom bars carry the span. Top bars carry the hogging moment over a continuous support, and a simple support still gets some top steel against partial fixity.

Switch landing B between shared and this flight alone, and watch F, M and the steel. Finish with the shared landing and every check passing. Try the example of WE 1.1 built into walls too.

Effective span on a landing: L = La + ½(Lb1 + Lb2), Lb ≤ 1.8 m (BS 8110 3.10.1.3). The landing strip inside the span carries nL (audit T-01). Open-well landing: wL = nLbL + VA, ML = wLLL²/8, K = 1.0. Shared landing: ½ nL (Mosley §8.8.2). Walls: κw = (bF − 150nw)/(bF + 75nw), for the flight and for a landing carried by this flight alone.

Course method of SEAA 4333 (notes and Mosley, Bungey & Hulse §8.8): loads per m² on plan; M = FL/10 with a continuous end, FL/8 with none; every check to EC2 (MS EN 1992-1-1). Geometry rules from the Uniform Building By-Laws 1984. The effective span on a landing and stairs built into walls follow BS 8110-1, 3.10.1.3 and 3.10.1.2, as the RC Design app uses them.
